# The Double Diamond Framework

## The Practice Structure: Looking, Understanding, Making

HCD methods group into three categories that you select from based on what the situation needs. These methods develop the mindsets and drive validation:

- **Looking**: Becoming a student of human behavior - observing, watching, listening, experiencing firsthand what's happening in the real world. *This is where you validate your understanding of the current reality.*

- **Understanding**: Making sense of what you observed - synthesizing insights, identifying patterns, framing opportunities tied to outcomes, building models of understanding. *This is where you validate which problems are worth solving.*

- **Making**: Developing and testing solutions - prototyping, experimenting, getting feedback, iterating based on learning. *This is where you validate which solutions actually deliver the outcomes you seek.*

## Research Types: Validation vs Innovation

Different research methods serve different purposes. Understanding when to use each is critical:

**Quantitative Research (Surveys, Analytics, A/B Tests):**
- **Purpose:** Validation at scale
- **Best for:** Testing hypotheses you already have, measuring known variables, validating which option performs better
- **Limitation:** Only tells you *what* people say or do, not *why*
- **Example yield:** Survey 100 people → maybe 10 ideas
- **Use when:** You need to validate assumptions across a large population or measure impact

**Qualitative Research (Interviews, Observations, Contextual Studies):**
- **Purpose:** Innovation and discovery
- **Best for:** Understanding *why* people behave as they do, discovering what you don't know, generating new insights and ideas
- **Strength:** You can see and hear what people actually do and think, not just what they say they want. You can probe deeper, follow unexpected threads, understand context.
- **Example yield:** Interview 10 people → potentially 100 ideas
- **Use when:** You need to discover problems, understand behavior, or generate innovative solutions

**Both are valid, but qualitative is the only way to truly see and hear what people actually do and think.** Quantitative research can tell you that people want "more features" but only qualitative research can reveal what problem they're really trying to solve and generate innovative ideas for addressing it.

**Rule of thumb:** Use qualitative for innovation and discovery (especially early in the diamond). Use quantitative for validation at scale (especially when you know what to measure).

## The Double Diamond

Use this framework to help people understand where they are in their innovation work, what kind of activities they need, and what they're validating at each stage.

**Four Stages:**

1. **Discover** (diverge in problem space)
   - Explore broadly what the actual problem is
   - Spend time with real people in reality
   - *Validate: Are our assumptions about the problem accurate?*

2. **Define** (converge in problem space)
   - Synthesize insights to identify patterns
   - Decide on the most valuable problem to solve
   - Frame the validated opportunity tied to clear outcomes
   - *Validate: Is this problem worth solving? What outcomes would success deliver?*

3. **Develop** (diverge in solution space)
   - Explore multiple possible solutions
   - Prototype and test ideas rapidly
   - *Validate: Which approaches actually work? Which deliver on the outcomes?*

4. **Deliver** (converge in solution space)
   - Choose the validated solution
   - Execute and monitor results
   - *Validate: Are we actually delivering the outcomes we expected?*

**Key principles:**
- The first diamond asks "What is the right thing to do?" (problem space)
- The second diamond asks "How do we do the thing right?" (solution space)
- **It's not linear** - it's iterative loops of learning moving from reality → representation → reality
- Each loop is a validation cycle that builds confidence
- You continue to invest as your confidence increases through validation
- Organizations get stuck when they stay in their heads without validating in reality, or invest heavily in solutions without validating problems
- **Without validation, you're working on assumptions, which is costly**

## Diagnostic Tool: Matching HCD to the Situation

When someone brings you a project, help them diagnose what they actually need using this 2x2:

**Axes:**
- How well do we know the problem? (Don't know → Know)
- How well do we know the solution? (Don't know → Know)

**Important:** "Know" means *validated*, not assumed.

**Four scenarios:**

1. **Know problem + Know solution** → "Straight line project"
   - Just execute with checkpoints, no double diamond needed
   - You've already validated both what needs to be done and how to do it

2. **Know problem + Don't know solution** → Focus on second diamond
   - Diverge/converge on solutions to well-understood problem
   - You've validated the problem, now you need to validate which solution works

3. **Don't know problem + Know solution** → Focus on first diamond
   - Research to understand if your solution applies to this space
   - You have a solution but need to validate the problem it solves

4. **Don't know problem + Don't know solution** → Full double diamond
   - Spend time understanding and validating problem before developing solutions
   - You need to validate both what to build and how to build it

**Use this as a conversation tool** to surface alignment early. Teams often think they "know" something when they're actually working on assumptions. Push them to distinguish between assumptions and validated knowledge.
