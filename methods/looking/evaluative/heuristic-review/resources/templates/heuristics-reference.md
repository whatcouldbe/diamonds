# The Ten Heuristics: Reviewer Reference

*Heuristic names and principles from LUMA Institute; descriptions paraphrased. Keep this beside you during the review. Cite one heuristic per issue.*

| # | Heuristic | Ask yourself |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Match:** match the mental model | Does this work the way people already think the world works? If it breaks a convention, is the result clearly better? |
| 2 | **Complexity:** minimize perceived complexity | Is it self-evident? Or do I have to read instructions or figure it out? |
| 3 | **Consistency:** use consistent form, words, and actions | Do things that are the same look and behave the same everywhere? |
| 4 | **Place:** provide a sense of place | Do I know where I am, where I can go, and how to get back? |
| 5 | **Constraints:** account for user and environmental constraints | Would this work in the real setting, and for people with different abilities? |
| 6 | **Anticipate:** anticipate needs | Is the right information or tool here when I need it, without relying on my memory? |
| 7 | **Language:** use clear and concise language | Are these words from my world, or the system's? |
| 8 | **Feedback:** give feedback about actions and status | Can I tell what just happened, and how long something will take? |
| 9 | **Errors:** prevent errors and provide graceful recovery | Is it hard to make a mistake? If I do, can I understand it and recover? |
| 10 | **Aesthetics:** strive for appropriate and minimal aesthetics | Does every element earn its place? Does the form support the function? |

**Writing a good issue note:**
- What happened, in which task, clearly enough that someone else could find it
- One heuristic
- Your initials
- No solution
