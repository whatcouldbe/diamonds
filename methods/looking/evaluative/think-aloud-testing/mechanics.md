# Think-Aloud Testing — Mechanics

A testing format where people narrate their own experience while performing a given task — their brain on speakerphone.
**Phase:** Looking | **Category:** Evaluative Research

---

## Purpose Statements

Use these in the introduction — lead with why, not what:

- Test the assumptions behind an idea by watching real people use it
- Gather honest, in-the-moment feedback — behaviour, not opinion
- Deepen the team's empathy by watching someone actually struggle or succeed
- Uncover opportunities for improvement you can't see from inside the work
- Lower development cost through early discovery — find the problem before you've built around it

---

## Inputs

- **The thing being tested** — a rough prototype at any fidelity, an existing experience, or a real product. Don't over-polish.
- **A few key tasks** — framed as something to accomplish (*"find a flight and book it"*), usually one at a time, though a task can be a whole flow.
- **Respondents** — 6–8 (up to ~10) real people, ideally matching the target user. Then diminishing returns.
- **A way to record** — screen and voice, always with permission.
- **A quiet place** — for the participant especially.
- **A test protocol** — the light intro/consent/task script (moderated or unmoderated; see `resources/templates/`).

---

## Outputs

- Recordings and transcripts of each session
- Observations coded across the experience (commonly Rose / Thorn / Bud, screen by screen)
- Patterns across respondents once clustered
- A prioritised backlog for the next iteration (when carried downstream)
- A team that has *watched* real use — deeper empathy and alignment

---

## Process

### Introduction

| Step | What to say |
|---|---|
| Frame the need | We've been working on [this thing] and we want to watch a few people actually use it before we go further |
| Name the benefit | Watching you use this — and hearing your thoughts as you go — shows us what works and what breaks in a way nothing else can |
| Confirm role & get consent | Confirm who they are; ask permission to record screen and voice |
| Set the tone | We're not testing *you* — there are no wrong answers. And I didn't make this, so you genuinely can't offend me |

### Instructions

| Step | What to say / do |
|---|---|
| Give the task | *"I'd like you to [task]."* Frame it as something to accomplish, not a feature to inspect |
| Ask them to think aloud | *"Keep your brain on speakerphone — narrate everything: what you see, what you expect, what's confusing."* (The hand-on-head gesture can help it land) |
| Name the one rule | *"I don't want you to silently click around — I really want you to think aloud. That's the whole thing."* |
| Set expectations on questions | *"If you ask me something, I might bounce it back to you — that's deliberate, not me being unhelpful"* |

### Execution

| Step | What to do / say |
|---|---|
| Go quiet | Stop talking. Listen, take good notes, record. Do **not** demonstrate or explain |
| Reflect questions back | *"How do you think you should proceed?"* / *"What do you think should be here?"* — never answer directly; defer direct questions to the end |
| Handle silence (lightly) | *"It looks like you're still engaging — can you tell me what you're thinking right now?"* then withdraw again |
| Handle unfinished parts | *"What did you expect to find here? What would be useful?"* |
| Capture two things separately | What they did/said (raw) vs. what the team interprets (kept apart) |

### End

| Step | What to do |
|---|---|
| Take held questions | Answer any direct questions you deferred to the end |
| Thank them | Their narration is a gift — close with genuine thanks |
| Confirm capture | Verify the recording saved and notes are legible for synthesis |

### Next Steps

| Step | What to do |
|---|---|
| Transcribe | Pull the transcript from the recording |
| Codify | Code the experience as Rose / Thorn / Bud, screen by screen |
| Cluster | Affinity-cluster the coded data into themes |
| Prioritise | Vote, or Bullseye-diagram the cluster headings; sort into a MoSCoW backlog |
| Iterate | Feed the prioritised backlog into the next version of the prototype |

## Stuff

- **Time:** A single session is often short (the worked-example assessment ran under 10 minutes); budget extra for briefing, consent, setup, and recording. Unmoderated briefs typically allow ~45 minutes.
- **The artifact:** The thing being tested — rough prototype, existing experience, or real product. Any fidelity.
- **Tasks:** A few key tasks written as goals to accomplish.
- **Recording kit:** Screen + voice capture (in-person, remote screen-share, or the participant's own recording). Permission first, always.
- **Quiet space:** Free of distraction, for the participant especially.
- **Protocol/script:** The light intro–consent–task script. Moderated and unmoderated versions in `resources/templates/`.
- **Respondents:** 6–8 (up to ~10), as close to the target user as you can get.
- **Preparation:** Recruit respondents, prepare the prototype and tasks, set up recording, ready the protocol.
