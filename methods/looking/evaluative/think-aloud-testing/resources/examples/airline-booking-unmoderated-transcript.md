# Example — Unmoderated Think-Aloud Test: Airline Booking

A real, unmoderated think-aloud test. The participant was asked to record their own experience attempting a task — finding and booking a flight — with no moderator present. They simply narrated as they went.

**Why this example matters:** it's the clearest illustration of *what good narration sounds like*. You can't fake this texture — the live frustration, the expectations, the moment trust breaks. It's also a clean example of the **unmoderated** variation (see `../templates/unmoderated-participant-brief.md`): notice there's no one nudging them, so the quality depends entirely on how well the brief set them up.

---

## The task

Find direct flights from the UK to Pittsburgh, under £800, travelling in September — having been told British Airways flies the route.

## What the narration surfaced

Just from listening — no survey, no rating — the findings are unambiguous:

- **The "Start planning your trip" CTA is a trap.** They click it expecting to book, land in a confusing content world (*"what is going on here?"*), and from then on actively *avoid* it (*"I'm not going to touch that again"*). A button doing the opposite of what it promises.
- **The "no direct flights for this route" message is where trust breaks.** They were told direct flights exist, so the message reads as simply *wrong* — and instead of saying which days *do* fly, it dead-ends them.
- **No flexible-date option.** *"In some travel sites you can click plus or minus a day… this is making me do it manually, which is annoying."*
- **Slow loads** add friction throughout.
- **The ending is the whole business case in one line:** *"this has been really frustrating… I'm going to go and look elsewhere."* Abandonment, narrated live.

You would never get *"the 'no direct flights' message made me abandon"* from a satisfaction score. That's the argument for the method in a single transcript.
