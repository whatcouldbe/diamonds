# Unmoderated Think-Aloud Test — Participant Brief

The script you give someone *ahead of* having them record their own think-aloud test in their own time (the airline-booking transcript in `../examples/` came from a brief like this).

In an unmoderated test there's no moderator in the room — so the brief has to carry everything a moderator normally would: the reassurance, the "narrate everything" instruction, *and* the anti-silence nudge, because no one is there to deliver them live. Read the brief aloud yourself once and it should feel warm, clear, and complete on its own.

---

## Ready-to-use brief

> Think-aloud testing is an easy way for you to share everything that comes to mind as you experience something new. It's like putting your brain on speakerphone and broadcasting your unfiltered thoughts. Seeing and hearing someone perform a task as they record it captures honest feedback in the moment — and that helps us quickly find ways to make this better.
>
> **First, the most important thing:** we're not testing *you* — there are no wrong answers — and you genuinely can't offend us. We didn't make this to show it off; we made it to learn. So please be completely honest. If something is confusing, annoying, or doesn't work, that's exactly what we want to hear.
>
> **For this activity you'll need:** a quiet place to work, the task or activity to complete, a way to record your screen and voice, and up to 45 minutes.
>
> **Here's what to do:** start recording your screen and voice, then complete the activity. While you do, say everything that pops into your head — it's like putting your mind on loudspeaker. Read content out loud as you try to understand it. As you go, talk through:
> - What do you find easy or helpful, and why?
> - What is difficult or confusing, and why?
> - What ideas do you have to make this experience better?
>
> **The one way to get this wrong is to go quiet.** If you notice you've stopped talking, that's your cue to start again — narrate even the boring bits.
>
> When you've finished, you'll have a screen recording of your honest feedback. You'll be ready to share it with [feedback@yourdomain.com].
>
> Ready? Prepare to record yourself, and get started.

## Notes on this template

This is the real WCB brief, with two deliberate additions the original lacked:

1. **The reassurance up front** — *"we're not testing you… you can't offend us."* In a moderated session the facilitator delivers this warmth live; in an unmoderated brief it has to be written in, or the participant self-censors.
2. **The explicit anti-silence line** — *"the one way to get this wrong is to go quiet."* This is the written stand-in for the moderator's gentle re-prompt. It's the single most important thing the brief does that a moderator would otherwise do in the room.

When you adapt it: keep the speakerphone metaphor, keep the three prompt questions (they're the method distilled), and make sure the destination for the recording is correct.
