# Moderated Think-Aloud Test — Protocol

A light protocol for a moderated session. It's lighter than an interview script because you're not working through a question set — you're handing someone a task and then getting out of the way. Adapt the bracketed parts; keep the moves.

> Generalised from the "Diagnostic Testing Protocol" used on the *What Could Be* project (see `../examples/`) and the practitioner's facilitation notes.

---

## 1. Open and confirm

> "Thanks for taking the time to speak with me today. I'm [name] — I [role / what I do]. And you're [confirm their name and role], is that right?"

> "I'm working on [the thing], and I'd like to get your feedback on an early version. In a moment I'll give you something to do."

## 2. Get consent to record

> "I'd like to record the screen and your voice as we go, so I can pay attention to you rather than scribbling notes — and so the team can learn from it afterwards. Is that okay?"

*(Record by default — but only with permission. If they decline, take written notes instead.)*

## 3. Set them at ease

> "Two things before we start. First — we're not testing *you*. There are no wrong answers, and anything you say is useful. Second — I didn't make this, so you genuinely can't offend me. Please be completely honest. If something's confusing or annoying, I want to hear it."

## 4. Explain think-aloud

> "The one thing I'll ask is that you keep your brain on speakerphone — narrate everything that's going through your head as you go. What you're looking at, what you expect to happen, what's confusing, what you'd do next. Even the boring bits."

> "And please don't go quiet and click around in silence — talking aloud is the whole point. We call it a think-aloud test."

*(Some moderators mime it: a hand on top of the head, moving as if thoughts are streaming out.)*

## 5. Give the task

> "Okay — here's what I'd like you to do: [the task, framed as a goal to accomplish — e.g. 'find a relevant flight from London to Pittsburgh in September and book it,' or 'use the tool to assess your readiness']."

*(One task at a time, though a task can be a whole flow.)*

## 6. Go quiet

Stop talking. Listen, take notes, let the recording run. Resist the urge to demonstrate or explain. Then:

**When they ask you a question** → reflect it back, don't answer:
> "How do you think you should proceed?" / "What do you think should be here?"

**When they go quiet but are still active** → re-enter small, then withdraw:
> "It looks like you're still engaging — can you tell me what you're thinking right now?"

**When they hit something not built yet** → turn it into a finding:
> "What did you expect to find here? What would be useful?"

**Direct questions you can't reflect** → defer:
> "Good question — let me come back to that at the end."

## 7. Close

> "That's everything I wanted to watch. [Answer any questions you held to the end.] Thank you — this was genuinely useful, and exactly what we needed."

---

## Moderator's reminders (don't read aloud)

- This is a test, not a demonstration. The instinct to explain is the trap. Hand over the task; get out of the way.
- Most of the fight against silence is won here, in the setup — not in the moment.
- Capture two things separately: what they did/said (their words) and what you interpret from it.
