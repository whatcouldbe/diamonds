# Critique — Mechanics

A structured protocol for getting balanced, constructive feedback on work-in-progress.
**Phase:** Looking | **Category:** Evaluative Research

---

## Purpose Statements

Use these in the introduction — lead with why, not what:

- Facilitate constructive discussion about work-in-progress
- Get feedback early and often — before attachment hardens
- Reveal blind spots the team can't see from inside the work
- Surface opportunities for improvement and prioritize what matters most
- Build organizational alignment — people who give feedback become invested

---

## Inputs

- **Something to share** — an artifact in any state: concept, wireframe, poster, journey map, plan, prototype. Any fidelity works.
- **A protocol** — the structured sequence of questions (posted visibly for the session)
- **Reviewers** — 5–7 people who know the challenge or are the people you're designing for. Closer to actual users = better. Further from the presenting team = more honest.
- **A way to capture feedback** — sticky notes (pink = strengths, blue = challenges, green = suggestions), a shared document, or a note-taker
- **Optional: color key posted visibly** — so reviewers know which sticky color maps to which feedback type

---

## Outputs

- Clarifying questions surfaced — gaps in understanding identified
- Strengths — what's working, what to preserve
- Challenges — risks, weaknesses, blind spots
- Suggestions — concrete ideas for improvement
- Unstructured notes organized by feedback type, ready for synthesis
- Increased alignment from reviewers who now understand and are invested in the work

---

## Process

### Introduction

| Step | What to say |
|---|---|
| Frame the need | We've been working on [this thing] and we want to get your honest feedback before we go further |
| Name the benefit | This conversation will help us see blind spots and figure out what's working and what isn't |
| Name the outcome | We'll walk away with clear feedback on strengths, challenges, and ideas for improvement |
| Set the tone | Your job is to give us honest feedback. Our job is to listen and collect — we're not here to defend, we're here to learn |

### Instructions

| Step | What to say |
|---|---|
| Explain the protocol | We're going to walk you through what we've been working on, then ask four types of questions — clarity, strengths, challenges, and suggestions |
| Set expectations | We may not have answers to every question, and that's okay. We're collecting information. |
| Silent write (if group) | For each question, take a minute to write your thoughts individually before we discuss as a group |
| Invite questions | Any questions before we get started? |

### Execution

| Step | What to do / say |
|---|---|
| Set context | *"The challenge we were trying to solve was..."* — explain where you are in the process and what you're addressing |
| Present the work | *"One of the ideas we had was..."* — walk reviewers through the artifact. Use softening language to reduce attachment and invite honesty. |
| Clarifying questions | *"Is there anything unclear about what I just presented?"* — let reviewers ask questions. Capture what you can't answer. Don't be defensive. |
| Strengths | *"What do you like about this concept?"* — collect positive feedback. If using stickies: pink. Silent write first, then discuss. |
| Challenges | *"What are some challenges we might face?"* — collect concerns and risks. If using stickies: blue. Silent write first, then discuss. |
| Build on it | *"How might we build on this idea?"* — collect suggestions. If using stickies: green. Silent write first, then discuss. |

### End

| Step | What to do |
|---|---|
| Thank reviewers | *"Thank you for your feedback"* — feedback is a gift. Close with genuine gratitude. |
| Summarize themes | Briefly play back the major themes you heard across all four categories |
| Confirm capture | Verify that notes / stickies are complete and legible for later synthesis |

### Next Steps

| Step | What to do |
|---|---|
| Synthesize | Cluster the feedback — consider affinity clustering to find themes |
| Prioritize | Use importance/difficulty or similar to decide which feedback to act on first |
| Iterate | Make changes to the artifact based on feedback before the next round |
| Schedule next round | Plan the next critique with a different group of reviewers — don't show the same version to more people |
| Share back | Consider sharing what you changed based on feedback — this reinforces that the reviewers' input mattered |

## Stuff

- **Time:** 20–45 minutes for a typical session
- **Protocol:** The 7-step critique protocol — printed or posted visibly in the room / shared digitally
- **Artifact:** The thing being critiqued — at any fidelity, in a format reviewers can see and engage with
- **Capture materials:** Sticky notes (pink, blue, green) and markers, or a digital equivalent. A shared document or board for the note-taker.
- **Reviewers:** 5–7 people per session. Multiple small rounds > one large review.
- **Color key:** Posted visibly — pink = strengths, blue = challenges, green = suggestions
- **Preparation:** Identify reviewers, prepare the artifact, print/post the protocol, set up the capture method
