# Critique — Recipes

How Critique combines with other methods. Recipes describe natural sequences — what typically comes before, what typically follows, and useful combinations for specific situations.

---

## Critique as the Universal Evaluator

Critique is unusual among HCD methods in that it is not phase-locked. It can follow *any* method that produces an artifact — a sketch, a concept poster, a journey map, a research plan, a prototype, a strategy document. The pattern is:

```
[Any method that produces an artifact] → Critique → Iterate → Critique again
```

If you have something to show, you can run a critique on it.

---

## Core Recipe: The Synthesis Flow

The most common sequence in Understanding and Define phases:

```
Research / Discovery
    ↓
Affinity Clustering — find themes in the raw data
    ↓
Critique — evaluate the clustering: does this framing make sense?
    ↓
Importance / Difficulty Matrix — prioritize which themes to act on
    ↓
Business case or road map
```

Critique sits in the middle of this flow as a checkpoint — an opportunity to get outside perspective on the synthesis before committing to priorities.

---

## Recipe: Critique Before You Build

Use Critique on early concepts or sketches before investing in prototyping. This is the highest-value application — the earlier you get feedback, the less attached you are to the work, and the more you can learn without cost.

```
Concept Ideation / Sketching
    ↓
Critique (rough artifacts, early reviewers)
    ↓
Iterate on concepts
    ↓
Critique again (refined concepts, closer to target users)
    ↓
Prototyping
```

---

## Recipe: Critique to Feed the I/D Matrix

When a critique session produces a large set of feedback items and you need to decide which to act on:

```
Critique → collect feedback (strengths, challenges, suggestions)
    ↓
Affinity Clustering → group similar feedback into themes
    ↓
Importance / Difficulty Matrix → prioritize which themes to address first
    ↓
Iterate on the artifact
```

The critique produces the raw material. The matrix produces the prioritized action list.

---

## Recipe: Peer Critique in a Working Session

When a team has been working individually in parallel and needs to converge:

```
Individual work (parallel) — each person develops their own version or concept
    ↓
Peer Critique — each person presents their work using the protocol
    ↓
Synthesis — identify what's working across all versions
    ↓
Combined concept or decision
```

Common in design studios, sprint reviews, and any context where divergent thinking needs to converge.

---

## Recipe: Critique as a Research Plan Check

Before running a major research effort (interviews, usability tests, surveys), critique the research plan itself:

```
Draft research plan (questions, participant criteria, method choice)
    ↓
Critique — with someone outside the research team
    ↓
Refine the plan
    ↓
Execute research
```

This is underused. Research plans carry assumptions that are hard to see from inside. A quick critique before fielding saves significant time and resources.

## Related Methods

| Method | Relationship |
|---|---|
| **Affinity Clustering** | Often follows Critique — cluster the feedback to find themes |
| **Importance / Difficulty Matrix** | Often follows Affinity Clustering → Critique — prioritize clustered feedback |
| **Rose / Thorn / Bud** | Similar structure (positive / negative / opportunity) but without the protocol and clarifying step. Critique is more rigorous; Rose/Thorn/Bud is faster and more informal |
| **Think-Aloud / Usability Testing** | Also evaluative, but observes behavior rather than soliciting structured opinion. Less simulatable, more behavioral. Use when you need to see how people *use* something rather than what they *think* about it |
| **Interviewing** | Closest relative — both follow a protocol and collect perspectives through structured conversation. Critique is focused on an artifact; interviewing is focused on a person's experience |
