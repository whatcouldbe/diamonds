# What's on Your Radar — References

## Participatory Design
What's on Your Radar sits within the broader tradition of participatory design — an approach that involves the people being designed for (or with) as active contributors to the design process, rather than passive subjects of research. Key contributors to this tradition include Pelle Ehn, who pioneered participatory design in Scandinavian systems design, and the broader participatory action research literature.

## Radar / Bullseye Diagrams
The radar format — concentric circles used to express proximity or importance — appears across multiple disciplines. In technology strategy, radar charts map technology readiness (the ThoughtWorks Technology Radar is a well-known example). In design, bullseye diagrams are used for stakeholder mapping and impact assessment. What's on Your Radar adapts this structure specifically for participatory prioritization, adding the segment dimension to represent distinct categories.

## Related Frameworks
- **Dot voting (dotmocracy):** A simpler group prioritization method. Faster but produces majority views rather than preserved individual perspectives.
- **Card sorting:** A participatory method where participants organize items into categories. Related in its individual-before-group logic but typically used for information architecture rather than strategic prioritization.
- **Rose / Thorn / Bud:** A simpler reflective framework for surfacing what's working, what isn't, and what has potential. Less structured than the radar but shares the participatory intent.
- **Stakeholder mapping:** Uses similar concentric-circle logic to express proximity and influence. Different purpose (mapping relationships) but the visual structure is closely related.
