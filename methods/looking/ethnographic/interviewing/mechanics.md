# Interviewing — Mechanics

A structured conversation to understand people's experiences, needs, and motivations.
**Phase:** Looking | **Category:** Ethnographic Research

---

## Purpose Statements

Use these in the introduction — lead with why, not what:

- Understand the experiences of the people we're designing for
- Challenge our assumptions with real perspectives
- Build empathy and deepen our understanding of people's needs
- Surface insights we wouldn't find through internal discussion alone
- Build credibility with customers and stakeholders by involving real people

---

## Inputs

- **A protocol** — a planned sequence of open-ended questions, organized from warm-up to deep exploration to wrap-up
- **An interviewee** — someone who represents the people you're designing for. Recruited in advance, confirmed, and consented.
- **A recording method** — audio recording (with permission) as backup to typed notes
- **A note-taker** — the interviewer can take notes, but a second person dedicated to notes is better
- **Context and framing** — a clear articulation of what you're trying to understand and why, prepared before the session

---

## Outputs

- Raw notes and/or recordings with key quotes captured
- Emerging themes and patterns across interviews
- Questions for further research
- Referrals — names of other people to interview
- Empathy and credibility — having spoken to the people you're designing for

---

## Process

### Introduction

| Step | What to say |
|---|---|
| Introduce yourself | Thanks for taking the time to speak with me today. My name is [name] and I'm working on [project context]. |
| State the purpose | I'd like to understand [what you're exploring]. Your perspective will directly inform how we approach this. |
| Set expectations | This should take about [time]. There are no right or wrong answers — I'm just interested in hearing about your experience. |

### Instructions

| Step | What to say |
|---|---|
| Ask for permission to record | Would you be willing to let me record our conversation? It's just for me to refer back to during synthesis. You may hear me typing notes as well. |
| Confirm time | Is [time commitment] still OK? |
| Invite questions | Any questions before we get started? |

### Execution

| Step | What to do |
|---|---|
| Warm-up | Start with easy questions anyone can answer: *"Tell me about your role"* or *"How long have you been doing [this work]?"* Build rapport. Get them talking. |
| Landscape | Ask about context and change: *"How has [topic] changed for you?"* *"What does a typical [day/week/process] look like?"* |
| Pain points | Ask about struggles and frustrations: *"What's the most frustrating thing about [topic]?"* *"Where do things break down?"* |
| Specific instance | Ask about a specific recent moment: *"Tell me about the last time you [did this thing]."* Use sub-questions to probe: What were you trying to accomplish? How did it go? What helped? What got in the way? |
| Topic deep-dives | Move into specific topic areas as needed. Signal transitions: *"Let's talk about [next topic]..."* |
| Magic button | *"If you could push a button and change one thing about [this], what would it do?"* Follow up: *"Why is that important?"* |
| Why | Throughout — use "why" to move from what people do to why they do it. This is the probe that turns surface answers into insights about underlying motivations. |

### End

| Step | What to do |
|---|---|
| Catch what you missed | *"Is there anything we haven't touched on that you'd like to share?"* |
| Ask for referrals | *"If I could only speak with one other person to gain better insight, who would that be?"* |
| Offer follow-up | *"Would it be OK to reach out again if I have follow-up questions?"* |
| Thank them | *"Thank you for your time — this is incredibly helpful."* |

### Next Steps

| Step | What to do |
|---|---|
| Debrief immediately | Within 15 minutes of the interview, capture your top-of-mind observations, surprising moments, and gut reactions. These fade fast. |
| Clean up notes | Fill in gaps while memory is fresh. Highlight key quotes verbatim. |
| Synthesize across interviews | After 3–5 interviews, begin affinity clustering to identify emerging themes. |
| Iterate the protocol | If early interviews reveal new topics or better questions, update the protocol for subsequent interviews. |
| Share raw insights | Share key quotes and emerging patterns with the team — don't wait until all interviews are complete. |

## Stuff

- **Time:** 30–60 minutes per interview (45 minutes is a good default). Budget 15 minutes before and after for setup and debrief.
- **Protocol:** A written sequence of open-ended questions — printed, on a screen, or on an index card. Keep it visible but don't read from it mechanically.
- **Recording:** Audio recording device or video call recording (with explicit permission). Essential backup to notes.
- **Notes:** Laptop, notebook, or shared document for real-time capture. Dedicated note-taker is ideal.
- **Participants:** One interviewer + one note-taker (optional but recommended). One interviewee. No more than two people from your side.
- **Environment:** Quiet space, minimal distractions. If remote, stable connection, cameras on if possible.
- **Number of interviews:** 5–7 per round for saturation. Multiple small rounds across the project, not one large batch.
