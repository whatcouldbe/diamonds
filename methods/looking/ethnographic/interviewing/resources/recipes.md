# Interviewing — Recipes

How Interviewing combines with other methods. Recipes describe natural sequences — what typically comes before, what typically follows, and useful combinations for specific situations.

---

## The Core Recipe: Discovery to Define

The most common sequence in the first diamond:

```
Secondary Research / Desk Research — what's already known
    ↓
Interviewing — 5–7 conversations with real people
    ↓
Affinity Clustering — find themes across interview notes
    ↓
Problem Framing — define the right problem to solve
```

Interviewing sits at the heart of discovery. It generates the raw data that everything downstream depends on.

---

## Recipe: Interview to Journey Map

When you want to map the full experience someone described:

```
Interviewing — ask people to walk through a specific experience
    ↓
Journey Mapping / Experience Diagramming — map the stages, touchpoints, emotions, and pain points they described
    ↓
Critique — present the journey map to stakeholders for feedback
```

The interview produces the narrative. The journey map makes it visual and shareable. The critique validates whether the map rings true.

---

## Recipe: Interview to Persona

When you want to create archetypes from real data:

```
Interviewing — 5–7 conversations across different types of users
    ↓
Affinity Clustering — group patterns in behavior, needs, and motivations
    ↓
Persona Development — synthesize patterns into representative archetypes
    ↓
Critique — present personas to the team and stakeholders for feedback
```

Personas built on real interview data are credible. Personas built on assumptions are fiction.

---

## Recipe: Stakeholder Interviews Before User Interviews

When you need organizational context before talking to users:

```
Stakeholder Interviews — understand the business context, constraints, and hypotheses
    ↓
Protocol Development — design user interview protocol informed by stakeholder perspectives
    ↓
User Interviews — talk to the actual people you're designing for
    ↓
Synthesis — affinity clustering, journey mapping, or problem framing
```

Stakeholder interviews are the same method with a different subject. They help you understand what the organization thinks is happening before you discover what's actually happening.

---

## Recipe: Interview → Critique → Iterate

When you want to close the loop between discovery and evaluation:

```
Interviewing — discover needs and pain points
    ↓
Synthesis + Concept Development — turn insights into concepts
    ↓
Critique — present concepts to people similar to those you interviewed
    ↓
Iterate — refine based on feedback
    ↓
Interview or Critique again — validate the iteration
```

This is the full discovery-to-evaluation loop. Interviewing starts it; Critique evaluates the output.

---

## Recipe: Simulated Interviews for Hypothesis Generation

When real access is limited or you need a head start:

```
Simulated Interviews (AI) — generate initial hypotheses about likely needs and pain points
    ↓
Protocol Refinement — use simulated responses to improve the protocol
    ↓
Real Interviews — validate or invalidate the hypotheses with actual people
    ↓
Synthesis — affinity clustering, journey mapping
```

Simulation generates plausible hypotheses. Real interviews validate them. Never skip the real interviews.

---

## Recipe: Practitioner Intake Interview

When a new HCD practitioner wants to contribute a method to the system:

```
Practitioner Intake Interview — the agent interviews the practitioner about their method
    ↓
Method File Generation — agent produces method.md, mechanics.md, teaching.md
    ↓
Critique — practitioner reviews and refines the generated files
    ↓
Resource Population — add tips, FAQ, examples, references
```

This is how Diamonds scales beyond a single author. The same interviewing skills applied to method codification.

## Related Methods

| Method | Relationship |
|---|---|
| **Observation / Fly on the Wall** | Complements interviewing — reveals what people do vs. what they say. Use when you suspect the say-do gap is significant. |
| **Contextual Inquiry** | Interviewing + observation, in context. "Show me" instead of "tell me." More resource-intensive but produces richer data. |
| **Try It Yourself / Walk a Mile** | Experience it yourself when you don't have access to users. Quick, cheap, but gives you your perspective rather than theirs. |
| **Focus Groups** | Group discussion vs. one-on-one. Faster but susceptible to social dynamics. Interviewing produces more honest individual perspectives. |
| **Surveys** | Quantitative complement. Surveys tell you how many; interviews tell you why. Often used together. |
| **Affinity Clustering** | The most common next step — group interview notes to find themes. |
| **Critique** | Related posture (receiving through a structured protocol) but different purpose — Critique evaluates an artifact; interviewing discovers experiences. |
