# Example Protocol: Self-Facilitated Templates

A real interview protocol used for discovery research on remote collaboration and self-facilitated templates. Demonstrates the full "I am / May I / Tell me / Anything else / Thank you" structure with topic sections, warm-up questions, and progressive depth.

**Context:** Discovery research for products to help teams focus on important project work and deliver better results in remote working sessions.

**Duration:** Less than 45 minutes

---

## The Protocol

### Introduction (I am...)

Thanks for taking the time to speak with me today. What Could Be are working on a number of products to help teams focus on their most important project work and deliver better results in remote working sessions. I'd like to understand your current role and what tools, processes, and challenges you encounter in daily project work.

### Permissions (May I...)

This interview should take less than 45 minutes to complete. Is that OK?

Would you be willing to record our conversation for the sole purpose of me being able to refer back to it as we synthesize our research? You may hear me typing notes in the background but a recording is a very helpful backup to have.

Thank you.

Any other questions before we get started?

### Core Questions (Tell me...)

**Warm-up and context:**
- Tell me about your role.
- How has working with others changed for you [since the pandemic]?

**Pain points:**
- What's the most frustrating thing with online collaboration?
- What do you continue to do, or insist on doing offline?
- What type of things do you do together (synchronous) vs. when you are apart (async)? Why is that?

**Specific instance:**
- Tell me about a recent working session you did with your teammates.
  - What were you trying to accomplish?
  - Who ran the session? How did that go?
  - What methods did you use? How did that go?
  - How did technology help or get in the way?

**Topic deep-dive — Activities and methods:**
- What activities or methods are critical to your work?
  - How often do you use these?
  - Which methods work best online? Why?
  - Which methods are most difficult to run online? Why?

**Topic deep-dive — Tools:**
- What's the most indispensable software tool you use? Why did you choose that?
- What tool do you despise but you continue to use?
- How do you manage information flow across different tools?

### Wrap-up

- Is there anything else you'd like to add?
- If I was only able to speak with one other person to gain better insight, who would that be?
- Would it be OK to invite your team to an activity they can do on their own time to provide additional input? What's the best way to make that happen?

Thanks again for your time.

---

## What to Notice About This Protocol

- **Warm-up is genuinely easy** — "Tell me about your role" requires zero recall and gets the interviewee talking
- **Moves from general to specific** — landscape questions first, then specific recent instances with pre-loaded sub-questions
- **Section transitions are explicit** — "Let's talk about activities and methods..." signals topic shifts
- **Sub-questions are pre-loaded but conversational** — under the "recent working session" question, the follow-ups are scripted but feel natural
- **"Who else" question is constrained** — "If I could only speak with one other person" forces the best lead
- **The closing recruits for the next method** — asking about a follow-up team activity is already planning the next research step
