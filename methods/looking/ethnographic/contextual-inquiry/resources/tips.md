# Contextual Inquiry — Tips & Advice

Drawn from three recorded sessions (2026): a practitioner teaching the method, a practitioner coaching a trainee who had just run one, and one field-tested retrospective walkthrough. Client specifics removed.

---

## Before

**Get as close to the real work as you can — and name the gap when you can't.** Experts who know a system well but don't do the job are a step removed. They're better than no one. Push for the real people in parallel.

**Check the logistics early.** If the people you need work on phones, in vehicles, or in the field, find out *before* the session whether they can share what they're doing, and on which platform. Use whatever is easiest for them.

**Think about who, deliberately.** Choosing less experienced participants gets fresh eyes; the most experienced show you the workarounds. Going to the extremes on purpose — least and most experienced, avoiders and enthusiasts — is a strong technique.

**Know what you're watching for.** "Watch everything" produces a lot of notes and little focus. Decide in advance which moments you most want to see.

## During

**Travel in pairs.** One asks, one documents. The second person catches what the lead misses — "take a picture of that."

**Let them work.** An occasional clarifying question is fine. Don't put words in their mouth, don't interpret, don't demo the "right" way.

**Watch their face.** People don't always say something is frustrating. If you see it, ask.

**Slow them down.** Experienced participants race across their own work. When you can't keep up, say so, and ask them to point to exactly where something is.

**Don't analyze out loud.** Notice, capture, and save the interpretation for later.

**Decline the homework.** If they offer to tidy up or annotate their work for you, say no — you want it as it was.

**Mind the observer effect.** If they know you as the expert, they may keep asking what *you* would do. Set the scene: you're there to learn from them.

## After

**Codify one item per sticky.** Rose, thorn, or bud. Place them against the step or screen they relate to.

**Write cluster titles as sentences.** "People don't realize financial help is available" is an insight; "finance" is a filing label.

**Expect improvement ideas.** Observing current work surfaces current pain, which pulls ideation toward incremental fixes. If you want transformative ideas, say so explicitly, and use an ideation method that releases current constraints.
