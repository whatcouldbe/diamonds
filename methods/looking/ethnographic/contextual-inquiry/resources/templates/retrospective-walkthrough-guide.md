# Retrospective Walkthrough — Guide Template · 45 min

> Generalized from one field-tested run (2026-09, n=1): a walkthrough of an internal scoring protocol the participant had built and used. Client specifics removed. Adapt the section names to the work you're studying; keep the time discipline.

**The move:** the participant drives the artifact they actually produced; you retrace the *actual* steps on it. "Show me where you did that" beats "talk me through your process."

**Leave with:**
1. **The current-state journey** — the real sequence of steps, start to finish.
2. **The essential-vs-specific line** — for each step: was this the *method*, or a choice made for this one instance?
3. **The divergences** — where the output disagreed with reality.

**Capture as you go — tag each note:** `[ESSENTIAL / INSTANCE-ONLY]` · `[FRICTION / WORKAROUND]` · `[REUSABLE ARTIFACT / TEMPLATE]`. Don't redesign in the room.

**Before the session:** list what prior write-ups claim about how the work was done (inputs, sources, steps). Every item on that list is something to *confirm*, not assume.

**Where the time goes:** the two segments only the participant can give you — how the output was really made (§2) and where it matched or missed reality (§5) — get 28 of the 45 minutes. Everything else stays brisk to protect them.

---

### 0 · Open — 2 min
- One line of framing: "I want to understand how you actually did this, step by step, on the artifact — the real, messy version. Not grading it, not fixing it today."
- Permission to record → ask them to share the artifact and start at the beginning.

### 1 · Trigger, prep & participants — 5 min *(brisk)*
- What kicked this off, and how did you prepare?
- Who was involved, and how were they chosen — principle, or who was available?
- *Capture the shape; don't dwell.*

### 2 · The core loop — 16 min *(the heart — go slow, let silences run)*
- Show me one real instance of the core step — what happened, in what order?
- Walk me through **one real conversion on the artifact, end-to-end**: this input → became this output.
- **Establish what was actually in play.** Ask outright what they drew on. Let them tell you what was and wasn't part of it. An absent input is itself a finding.
- For each input they confirm: where did it enter, and when inputs disagreed, which won?
- *Flag every judgment call, every "it depends," every shortcut.* Keep pulling them back to specific points on the artifact.

### 3 · Synthesis → output — 5 min *(brisk)*
- Walk me *up* the artifact — how does all this roll into the final output?
- What did the output look like, who got it, and which parts are reusable structure vs one-off?

### 4 · Stakeholders, follow-up, lessons — 4 min *(brisk)*
- Where did others come in, and what did it change?
- If you ran it again tomorrow — keep, drop, change?

### 5 · Validity read + close — 12 min *(protect this)*
- Anchored on the artifact: **where did the output agree with what you actually saw — and where did it diverge?** Don't accept "it mostly matched"; find the specific places.
- Was there a case you *felt* was good or bad that the output disagreed with? What did you do?
- *Sit in the divergences* — they're often the single most valuable thing the session produces.
- Close: "What haven't I asked that I should have?" → thank them → confirm follow-ups.

---

**Time discipline:** §2 and §5 are ring-fenced. Running long? Cut depth from §1, §3, §4 — you can reconstruct those later from the artifact. You can't reconstruct the divergences.
