# Value Proposition Poster — Recipes

The poster is a **packaging-and-rationale** method. It assumes you already have an idea worth backing, and its job is to make that idea travel, get pressure-tested, and earn support. That places it late in the Develop space, between having a chosen concept and committing resources to build it.

## What comes before

- **Round Robin** / **Creative Matrix** → generate and develop candidate concepts. The poster carries the winner, not the raw set.
- **Importance/Difficulty Matrix** / bullseye diagramming → prioritise which idea earns a poster. Don't poster everything; poster the bet.
- **Abstraction Laddering** / **Statement Starters** → if the framing or the "who and what" is still fuzzy, sharpen it before the statement, or the statement will be soft.

A good signal you're ready: you can name a customer, a job to be done, and a pain/gain worth addressing. If you can't, go back upstream.

## The core sequence

```
Prioritised concept
   → Value Proposition Poster   (statement + storyboard + assumptions)
   → Critique                   (structured feedback on the finished poster)
   → pick the first element to prototype
   → Rough & Ready Prototyping  (build the minimal piece)
   → validate the promoted assumptions (Interviewing, Think-Aloud Testing)
```

## What comes after

- **Critique** + **Rose, Thorn, Bud** → the poster is *built* to hand off to a feedback round. The four prompts (clarifying / strong / challenges / build-on) layer directly onto it.
- **Rough & Ready Prototyping** → the most important downstream move. After critique, the idea is almost always too big to prototype whole, so the team points at one element of the poster — a storyboard panel or a promoted assumption — and builds the minimal version of *that* first.
- **Hypothesis Statements** → formalise the promoted assumptions into testable hypotheses.
- **Interviewing** / **Think-Aloud Testing** → the methods that actually validate the assumptions the poster surfaced. The poster names the bets; these settle them.

## When *not* to reach for it

- The idea hasn't been chosen yet — you're still diverging. Use ideation/prioritisation first.
- You haven't talked to anyone and have no sense of the real pain — the poster will look polished but rest on air. Do some Looking first; a confident poster on an unvalidated problem is exactly the trap Diamonds exists to prevent.
- The audience needs depth, not altitude (e.g. a technical design review). The poster is for busy stakeholders deciding whether to back something, not for the team executing the build.
