# Workshop Arc — Value Proposition Poster at scale

The arc of a facilitated session in which multiple teams each build a poster for their own idea, then review each other's work. Transcribed and anonymised from a real enterprise workshop; the client is referred to throughout as the placeholder enterprise **Bankeo**, and all individuals are removed.

This is the multi-team variation of the method — useful inside an organisation running an internal idea-submission or innovation programme, where many teams develop proposals in parallel.

---

## Goals of the session

State these up front — lead with why:

- Write a compelling value proposition statement
- Succinctly describe how your idea works in practice, for the benefit of the end-user
- Capture key questions and assumptions about your idea's desirability, feasibility, and viability
- Receive constructive feedback from colleagues
- Consider that feedback to develop your proposition and improve your proposal

---

## Agenda

1. Welcome, goals & orientation
2. Create value proposition statements
3. *(short break)*
4. Draft a storyboard of how it works
5. *(short break)*
6. Capture questions & assumptions
7. Get feedback on your proposition
8. Consider next steps

---

## A useful opening check-in

Before starting, take the room's temperature with a single question and a 1–5 reply in the chat:

> **"How prepared are you to present your idea in 2 minutes?"**
> 5 — Ready and confident · 4 — Need final tweaks · 3 — Want to refine and practice · 2 — Rough outline, lots still to do · 1 — Starting from zero

This both sets the three-minute-pitch expectation early and tells the facilitator where each team is starting from.

---

## The peer-feedback round (the multi-team mechanic)

Each team builds its poster, then teams give each other feedback:

1. Each team places a copy of its **latest poster** into the shared feedback space (a designated slide / board frame).
2. Each team is assigned **another team's poster** to review.
3. Reviewers visit that poster, read the work in progress, and leave feedback using the stickies — one sticky per item, initialled, placed on the part of the poster it refers to.
4. The four prompts: *What clarifying questions do you have? · What is strong, and why? · What challenges might this face, and why? · How might we build on or strengthen it?*

The facilitator confirms each team has posted before the round starts, and gives the feedback instructions in the chat so they're available throughout.

---

## Beyond today — four things to do next

Close by pointing forward:

1. Continue developing your poster with your team
2. Work with your coach
3. Share your poster with stakeholders for feedback
4. Repeat until submission time

You'll leave with: a poster that describes how value is created for a customer and how they'll experience it; a set of assumptions to test and key questions to answer; and a set of feedback stickies suggesting improvements. You'll be ready to gather more feedback, iterate, and begin validating the assumptions — including choosing the one element to prototype first.
