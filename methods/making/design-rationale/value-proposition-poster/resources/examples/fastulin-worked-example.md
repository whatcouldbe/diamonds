# Worked Example — Fastulin

A complete, well-formed Value Proposition Poster, transcribed from a real teaching artifact. *Fastulin* (a rapid insulin-heating device) and *Claudia* (the storyboard persona) are fictional teaching inventions and are kept as-is. The reviewer initials *SM* / *DT* on the feedback are the method's own authors. No real company or individual appears.

Use this as the **quality bar**: it shows what "good" looks like in each zone.

---

## Zone 1 — Value proposition statement

> **Fastulin**
>
> **Our** rapid insulin heating device
> **help(s)** type 1 diabetes patients
> **who want to** safely inject insulin quickly
> **by** minimising waiting time
> **and guarantee** room temperature insulin when it's needed

*Why it works:* a specific segment (type 1 diabetics), a concrete job (inject quickly and safely), a real pain removed (waiting time), and a tangible gain (guaranteed room-temperature insulin on demand).

---

## Zone 2 — How it will work (storyboard, six panels)

1. Claudia's glucose level is too high and she needs to administer insulin quickly — but it takes up to 30 minutes for insulin to reach the correct temperature straight from the fridge.
2. Claudia takes a vial of insulin from her fridge and places it in the Fastulin device, then presses the green button to start the process.
3. The device counts down from 2 minutes as it heats up the insulin.
4. Once the device has counted down, an alarm goes off to notify Claudia that the insulin is ready to use.
5. Claudia administers the insulin and checks her glucose levels.
6. Claudia has been able to administer room-temperature insulin in a fraction of the time she would normally have taken, and is ready for her day.

*Why it works:* one named, believable character; a genuine problem in panel 1; a clear problem-to-transformation arc; experience, not engineering.

---

## Zone 3 — Key questions and assumptions

Framed by the three dimensions — *How desirable is this concept? How feasible is it to build? How viable is this from a business perspective?*

**Desirability**
- Would reaching the correct insulin temperature in under two minutes be useful?
- How much of a problem is incorrect insulin temperature for type 1 diabetes patients?
- What hacks do patients currently use to get insulin to room temperature?

**Feasibility**
- Is it possible to heat insulin to room temperature in under 2 minutes?
- Can heating insulin in two minutes be done safely?
- What technology already exists to heat liquids/medicines?
- Has anything like this been tried before? What other industries can we learn from?

**Viability**
- What is the potential market for this product?
- What might it cost to develop a prototype?
- Is there potential to develop a service off the back of it?

*Note the integrity of this zone:* two of the questions ("is it possible…", "can it be done safely…") are genuine deal-breakers. The poster puts them in plain sight rather than hiding them.

---

## Layer 4 — Critique feedback (added by reviewers)

When the poster went through a Critique round, reviewers (SM, DT) added initialled stickies, placed on the relevant zone and grouped by the four prompts:

**Clarifying questions**
- What size is the Fastulin device likely to be? *(SM)*
- How many people reported waiting-time frustration during research? *(DT)*
- Why do you need to keep insulin in a fridge? *(SM)*
- What is the smallest microwave in production? *(DT)*

**What's strong, and why (Rose)**
- Heating insulin in under two minutes would be a game changer. *(SM)*
- 30 mins down to 2 mins is a really strong benefit! *(DT)*
- Good to have an alarm — helps people who are visually impaired. *(SM)*

**Challenges it might face, and why (Thorn)**
- I'm worried the insulin might not be safe to use after heating. *(SM)*
- Is another piece of kit going to create another headache? *(DT)*
- A big device will clutter up a small kitchen. *(SM)*

**How to build on or strengthen it (Bud)**
- Add the two-minutes heating time to the value proposition statement. *(SM)*
- What else might the device do? What could it connect to? *(DT)*
- Could the device double up as packaging? *(DT)*
- Could exothermic heating be used — maybe even as part of the packaging? *(DT)*
- Make the device look nice and not medical — if Apple designed it, what might it look like? *(SM)*
- How about "Instulin" as a different name? *(SM)*
- Could there be a food / retail application? *(DT)*

---

## What happens next

Reading the feedback, the obvious **first thing to prototype** isn't the whole device — it's the riskiest, most uncertain element the critique kept circling: *can insulin be heated to room temperature in under two minutes, safely?* That single feasibility bet is what to make and test first, which is the handoff into Rough & Ready Prototyping. The "build-on" stickies (packaging, naming, connectivity) are parked as later bets, not first ones.
