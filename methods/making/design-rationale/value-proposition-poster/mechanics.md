# Value Proposition Poster — Mechanics

A single-page format that makes a developed idea understandable and compelling to a busy audience: a value proposition statement, a storyboard of the idea in a customer's hands, and the key questions and assumptions that admit what isn't known yet. Built to be pitched in three minutes and to set up structured feedback.
**Phase:** Making | **Category:** Design Rationale

---

## Purpose Statements

Use these in the introduction — lead with why, not what:

- Make a new idea easy to understand and exciting in three minutes — without the deck and without the research pile
- Turn an abstract proposition into something people can see, by showing it in a real customer's hands
- Show, honestly, what you don't yet know — so the room helps you improve it rather than just judging it
- Build the raw material for a business case, a pitch to decision-makers, and a roadmap for what to do next
- Set up a Critique round and surface the one element worth prototyping first

---

## Inputs

- **A developed, ideally prioritised idea** — a concept worth backing, not a raw brainstorm (often from Round Robin / Creative Matrix, prioritised via Importance/Difficulty)
- **The poster template** — statement frame, six-panel storyboard, assumptions area
- **Icons / simple imagery** — for the storyboard panels
- **Sticky notes** — for assumptions and for feedback
- **Optional: an AI assistant** — to pressure-test each zone (see `resources/ai-usage.md`)

---

## Outputs

- **A one-page Value Proposition Poster** — pitchable in ~3 minutes
- **A prioritised set of assumptions / key questions** — the deal-breakers, ready to validate
- **Feedback stickies** — clarifying / strong / challenges / build-on
- **A named first element to prototype** — the minimal piece to build rough and test

---

## Process

### Introduction

| Step | What to say |
|---|---|
| Frame the problem | "You can't pitch an idea with a sticky note, and you can't pitch it with a hundred pages of research. This is the page in between." |
| Name the three zones | "One page: what the idea is and who it's for, a short story of it in a customer's hands, and an honest list of what we still need to find out." |
| Set the three-minute bar | "The goal is to pitch this in three minutes. You will want to say everything. Don't — the format earns the questions." |
| Set the tone on assumptions | "Naming what you don't know isn't a weakness here. It's what makes a backer trust you and lean in." |

### Instructions

| Step | What to say |
|---|---|
| Walk the statement | "Fill in: *Our [X] helps [who] who want to [job] by [removing pain] and [creating gain].* Be specific — a named segment, a measurable outcome." |
| Walk the storyboard | "Answer six questions as one-line captions, about one named customer, from problem to transformation. Words first, icons second, images only if there's time." |
| Walk the assumptions | "What are we assuming about desirability, feasibility, viability? One per sticky. The deal-breakers go on the poster." |
| Explain the feedback round | "When posters are ready, you'll review each other's: clarifying questions, what's strong, what's challenging, how to build on it — initial your stickies and place them where they apply." |

### Execution

| Stage | Time (guide) | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| **Statement** | draft 10 / test 15 / revise 10 | Fill the mad-lib, pressure-test for specificity and differentiation, revise |
| **Storyboard** | 6 questions 15 / icons 10 / refine 15 / imagery 10 | Answer the six questions, add icons, refine the narrative, upgrade imagery if time |
| **Assumptions** | generate 10 / prioritise 5 | Surface questions across desirability/feasibility/viability, promote the deal-breakers |
| **Assemble** | — | Bring the three zones onto one page; check it reads in three minutes unaided |

### End

| Step | What to do |
|---|---|
| Pitch | Each author presents their poster in ~3 minutes — pulling back from saying everything |
| Critique round | Reviewers add initialled feedback stickies (clarifying / strong / challenges / build-on), placed where they apply |
| Author listens | The author's job is to receive, not defend — the assumptions zone already licensed honesty |

### Next Steps

| Step | What to do |
|---|---|
| Fold feedback in | Strengthen weak areas, answer clarifying questions, add new assumptions |
| Pick the first prototype | Point at one element of the poster — a storyboard panel or a promoted assumption — and name the minimal piece to build first |
| Carry forward | Into Rough & Ready Prototyping (build the element), Hypothesis Statements (formalise assumptions), and validation (Interviewing, Think-Aloud Testing) |
| Keep it living | Re-share the improved poster with stakeholders; it's the artifact that travels |

## Stuff

- **Time:** a focused build runs roughly 90 minutes to a first complete poster; statement-only can be done in ~30. The poster then improves over days as it's shared.
- **The poster template** — statement frame + six-panel storyboard + assumptions area (one slide / one large sheet)
- **Icons and simple images** — for the storyboard
- **Sticky notes** in two roles — assumptions (by the team) and feedback (by reviewers); reviewers initial theirs
- **Optional: an AI assistant** — stage prompts in `resources/ai-usage.md`
- **A named customer** — decided before the storyboard starts; "the user" won't do
