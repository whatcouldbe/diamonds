# Hypothesis Statements — Tips

`[Reconstructed throughout — derived from the method's lineage and the quality bar in the worked example, not from facilitation experience. Flagged so a practitioner can correct or confirm.]`

- **The unfalsifiable statement is the enemy.** If a team can't imagine the metric coming back disappointing, the statement isn't done. Make "what number would prove us wrong?" the recurring question.
- **Feature ≠ outcome.** "We believe adding search will help users search" is circular. The outcome is the change in the person's world, not the mechanism.
- **The high-level goal gets rushed.** It's last and feels optional. It isn't — it's the clause that makes the bet matter. Protect time for it.
- **Specific personas or it doesn't count.** "Users" can't be recruited. "Busy parents," "novice cooks" can. The example holds this bar consistently — match it.
- **Date and attribute the board.** The worked example treats it as "hypotheses as of [date]." Hypotheses expire; a board that looks permanent stops getting revisited.
