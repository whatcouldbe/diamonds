# Creative Matrix — Mechanics

A structured ideation method that generates a large volume of new and unusual ideas quickly by sparking thinking at the intersections of a grid — people-related columns and enabling-solution rows — in a silent, time-boxed sprint.
**Phase:** Making | **Category:** Concept Ideation

---

## Purpose Statements

Use these in the introduction — lead with why, not what:

- Generate a large volume of new and unusual ideas quickly, without the dynamics that distort traditional brainstorming
- Make sure every voice in the room contributes — the silent mechanic democratises participation
- Use structure to spark unusual combinations that free-form brainstorming wouldn't produce
- Create a shared pool of ideas the whole team has contributed to before evaluation begins

---

## Inputs

- **A clearly framed challenge** — overarching How Might We or problem statement; sits in the top-left cell of the grid
- **A populated grid** — column headings (people-related) and row headings (enablers) agreed before the sprint; bottom row always Wild Card
- **Sticky notes** (physical or digital) — one idea per sticky
- **Pens or markers** — thick enough to be legible; digital: emojis supplement drawings
- **Timer**
- **Optional: Statement Starters output** — if upstream HMW questions exist, these become column headings

---

## Outputs

- **A populated matrix** — a large volume of ideas at the intersections of the grid
- **A shortlist** — 8–12 ideas selected through individual nomination, dot voting, or affinity clustering
- **Input for prioritisation** — the shortlist feeds into Importance/Difficulty Matrix, Critique, or concept development

---

## Process

### Introduction

| Step | What to say |
|---|---|
| Show the grid | Point out the main challenge (top-left), column titles (people), row titles (enablers), and Wild Card row (bottom). |
| Frame the challenge | "Everything we generate today connects back to this: [read the main challenge]." |
| Ask about brainstorming | "Has anyone been in a brainstorming session? How did it go?" Acknowledge positives; name the common failure modes. |
| Name the antidote | "This method is structured, silent, and fast. You'll all contribute at the same time. No one waits for permission, no voice dominates." |

### Instructions

| Step | What to say |
|---|---|
| One idea per sticky | "Each sticky is one idea — not a category, not a theme. One specific idea." |
| Work at the intersections | "Find a column and a row. Generate the idea that lives where they meet. Place the sticky in that cell." |
| Write enough context | "Write enough that someone who wasn't in this room could understand your idea without asking you." — Show examples: weak ("app") vs. strong ("smart watch notification when it's time for medication, with a pill emoji"). |
| Keep moving | "If you're stuck on a cell, move to another one. Come back later. Read other people's stickies to spark new ideas." |
| Set the tone | "Wild ideas are welcome. You can tame a wild duck — you can't make a tame duck wild. Quantity over quality. There are no bad ideas." |
| Name the silence | "We work in silence. No discussion. Think it, write it, stick it." |

### Execution

| Phase | Time | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| **Ideation sprint** | Up to 10 min (8 "movie minutes" for urgency) | Silent simultaneous ideation. Facilitator watches for low-context stickies and coaches quietly if needed. |
| **Optional: Alternative Worlds injection** | +3 min | Mid-sprint, name an inspiring organisation or person: "For the next three minutes, imagine you're [Patagonia / Apple / SpaceX]. What would they generate?" |

### End

| Step | What to do |
|---|---|
| Read-through | Give approximately 2 minutes per column. Team reads silently as a group. Only permitted conversation: clarification — "I don't understand this idea." No critique, no evaluation. |
| Select or prioritise | Choose the approach based on time and next steps — see selection options below. |

**Selection options:**

| Approach | When to use | How it works |
|---|---|---|
| **Individual nomination** | When feeding into Importance/Difficulty Matrix | Each person picks 2 ideas using agreed criteria (e.g. bold, strategically connected, willing to champion). Team of 4–5 produces 8–10 ideas. |
| **Dot voting** | Quick visual preference signal | 3 dots each, placed on any idea including your own. |
| **Affinity clustering** | When many ideas are related | Group related stickies regardless of grid position; name the cluster. Typically yields 6–12 overarching ideas. |

### Next Steps

| Step | What to do |
|---|---|
| Capture the shortlist | Document the selected ideas with enough context to carry them into the next session |
| Note strong themes | Ideas that appeared in multiple cells or were selected by multiple people signal strong opportunity areas |
| Carry forward | The shortlist becomes input for Importance/Difficulty Matrix, Critique, or concept development (e.g. Value Proposition Poster) |

## Stuff

- **Time:** 45–60 minutes for a full session including introduction, sprint, read-through, and selection
- **The grid** — large format (physical poster or digital board); 5×5 is standard
- **Sticky notes** — plentiful; one idea per sticky
- **Thick pens or markers** — legibility matters when ideas are read back across the grid
- **Timer** — enforce the sprint length
- **Optional: Alternative Worlds prompt card** — list of inspiring organisations or individuals to introduce mid-sprint
- **Digital:** Miro, Mural, FigJam; emoji palette open and ready
