# Creative Matrix — Enabler Library

Enablers are the rows of the Creative Matrix. Each one represents a category of activator — something you could deploy, leverage, or partner with to solve the problem in the column. The best enablers create productive tension with the columns: slightly uncomfortable choices produce wilder ideas than safe, familiar ones.

Every enabler is more useful when accompanied by sub-examples — specific things within the category that give participants something concrete to grab onto. These sub-examples function as "think about" prompts.

---

## The Default Four

Use these as the starting set when context doesn't drive a more specific choice. They work across almost any challenge type.

### Technology & Digital Media
Mobile devices, wearable tech, social media, gaming and simulations, lifestyle tracking, embedded sensors, generative AI, internet of things.

### Games & Competitions
Motivations, rewards, badges, points, prizes, teamwork, scoring, leaderboards, peer competition, cooperative challenges.

### People & Partnerships
Strategic partnerships, companies and their leaders, spokespeople, evangelists, peer communities, co-creation with customers, celebrity or expert association.

### Events & Programs
Meet-up events, conferences, symposiums, workshops, courses of study, peer-to-peer forums, communities of practice, recurring programming.

## Extended Enabler Set

Draw from these based on context. Look for what creates genuine tension with your columns — and consider what's currently de rigueur in the industry or culture.

### Public Policies & Laws
Policy positions, prospective legislation, unwritten customs and traditions, regulatory frameworks, institutional and individual roles.

### Hotspots & Hangouts
Locations of daily activity, high-traffic areas, common gathering places, online communities and destinations.

### Internal Policies & Procedures
Company guidelines, incentives and rewards, training and education programs, existing workflows and governance.

### Facilities & Environments
Permanent structures, temporary installations, virtual worlds, mobile environments, physical or digital spaces where the experience happens.

### Surprise & Provocation
Unexpected experiences, pop-up activations, guest appearances, moments that break routine and create memorable contrast.

### Lifestyle Tracking
Nutrition, physical activity, sleep quality, the quantified self — data about personal behaviour used to personalise or prompt.

### Shows & Videos
Live performances, broadcast media, public service content, viral video formats, storytelling through moving image.

### Celebrities & Superstars
Famous entertainers, celebrated athletes, historical figures, hometown heroes — people whose association carries meaning for an audience.

### Engage All Senses
Taste, touch, smell, sight, hearing — designing for sensory experience beyond the visual default.

### Accessories
Thematic accessories, cases and covers, connectors and mounts, fashion — physical objects that extend or personalise a product or experience.

### Physical Variation
Different sizes, forms and shapes, unusual materials, textures and finishes — changing the physical object itself.

### Welcoming & Onboarding
First impressions, greetings, establishing community, reducing the friction of beginning — the front door of any experience.

### Personalisation
Tailored recommendations, adapting to situations and behaviour, user profiles — making something feel made for this person.

### Sustainable Practices
Reducing, reusing, recycling, circular design — solutions that minimise environmental impact or make sustainability part of the value proposition.

### Responsible AI
Machine learning, automation, AI-assisted personalisation, human-AI interaction design, transparency and explainability.

## Experience-Specific Enabler Sets

Use these when columns are stages of an experience (e.g. the Five E's: Entice, Enter, Engage, Exit, Extend).

### The Theatrical Model
Think of delivering an experience as putting on a play:
- **Actors** — your product, service, or people who deliver it
- **Audience** — your users or customers
- **Stage** — where the interaction takes place (physical, digital, or hybrid)
- **Script** — what is said or communicated at each moment
- **Backstage** — what needs to happen behind the scenes to make the front-of-house experience work

### RATER (Service Quality Dimensions)
The five dimensions people use to evaluate any experience:
- **Reliable** — the experience works consistently; errors are rare and recovery is graceful
- **Assured** — trust is established and reinforced throughout
- **Tangible** — the physical or visible elements that make quality perceivable
- **Empathetic** — needs are understood and reflected back; people feel seen
- **Responsive** — interactions acknowledge people appropriately; responsiveness is about fit, not just speed

When columns are experience stages and rows are RATER dimensions, the Creative Matrix functions as a service blueprint — mapping what quality looks like at every stage.

## Capability-Building Enabler Set

Use when the challenge is about building skills, knowledge, or behaviours within an organisation.

### Training
Workshops, classes, micro-learning, in-person or remote delivery, live or self-paced, role-based, generic or situation-specific.

### Support
Peer coaching, expert mentoring, communities of practice, in-person or online channels, networks, events, councils, search and bots.

### Resources
Recipes, templates, frameworks, kits, digital tools, planning materials, handbooks, field guides.

### Assessment
Formal and informal evaluations, surveys and benchmarks, individual or team measurement, reports, dashboards, credentials, certifications.
