# Round Robin — Mechanics

A structured ideation method in which ideas are passed from person to person through three rounds — generate, critique, resolve — building shared ownership and pressure-testing ideas before anyone commits to a direction.
**Phase:** Making | **Category:** Concept Ideation

---

## Purpose Statements

Use these in the introduction — lead with why, not what:

- Generate ideas that are stronger because they've already survived critique
- Build shared ownership — by the end, no idea belongs to the person who started it
- Make sure every voice in the room contributes, regardless of group size or seniority
- Break attachment to ideas so the team can evaluate them honestly

---

## Inputs

- **A problem statement or prompt** — a How Might We question, statement starter, or challenge the team is solving for. Everyone works from the same prompt.
- **One template per participant** — paper (folded), digital frame (Miro/Mural), or large-format poster
- **Timer** — rounds are timed; ratio below
- **Optional: Rose/Thorn/Bud framework** — useful structure for the critique pass

---

## Outputs

- **Resolved concepts** — ideas that have been generated, critiqued, and developed by three different people
- **A critique record** — the specific failure modes identified during the critique pass
- **Shared ownership** — the output belongs to the room, not to individuals

---

## Process

### Introduction

| Step | What to say |
|---|---|
| Frame the prompt | "We're all solving for the same thing — [read the prompt]. Everyone will work from this." |
| Name the mechanic | "We're going to run three rounds. You'll generate an idea, pass it, critique someone else's idea, pass it again, then resolve the critique on the idea you inherit." |
| Name what's different | "We're deliberately breaking two brainstorming rules: we're going to judge ideas, and we're going for one strong idea each — not a hundred." |
| Set expectations | "You'll give your idea away after the first round. By the end, no one owns the idea they started with — and that's the point." |

### Instructions

| Step | What to say |
|---|---|
| Show the template | Walk through the three sections: generate, critique, resolve. On paper, they're folded — only open the section you're working on. |
| Explain the passing | "After each round, pass your template to the next person. [Explain direction — right, to another table, next digital frame, etc.]" |
| Set the tone for critique | "In round two, be honest. List every way this idea could fail. Vague critique produces vague resolution — be specific." |
| Set the tone for resolve | "In round three, your job is to address the critique. Strengthen what's weak, fix what's broken, develop what's promising. You're evolving the idea, not replacing it." |

### Execution

| Round | Time | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| **Generate** | Longest (e.g. 10 min) | Each person develops their best response to the prompt. Name it, sketch it, explain the key elements. Then pass. |
| **Critique** | ~Half of generate (e.g. 5 min) | Each person lists all the ways the inherited idea could fail. Rose/Thorn/Bud is a useful structure. Then pass. |
| **Resolve** | ~Double generate (e.g. 10–15 min) | Each person addresses the critique. Fix failures, reinforce weaknesses, develop the promising parts. |

### End

| Step | What to do |
|---|---|
| Share out | Each participant presents the resolved idea they finished — not the one they started. The presenter is usually not the originator. |
| Surface the critique | Name what challenges were identified and how they were addressed. This is part of the story of the idea. |
| Move to evaluation | Use an evaluative method (Importance/Difficulty Matrix, dot vote, Critique) to prioritize which resolved concepts to develop further. |

### Next Steps

| Step | What to do |
|---|---|
| Capture the resolved concepts | Document each idea with its name, the original concept, the critique, and the resolution |
| Note recurring critique themes | If the same failure mode appeared across multiple ideas, it may be a constraint worth naming explicitly |
| Carry forward | The strongest resolved concepts become briefs for prototyping or further development |

---

## Stuff

- **Time:** 25–40 minutes minimum for three rounds; scale up for more complex prompts or more participants
- **One template per participant** — paper (folded into thirds), Miro/Mural digital frame, or large-format poster
- **Timer** — enforce round lengths
- **Optional: Rose/Thorn/Bud reference** — for the critique pass
- **Optional: Sticky notes or markers** — if working on printed or poster templates
- **Luma Workplace** has a standard paper template; custom templates can be created for non-ideation applications (role definitions, mission statements, concept posters)
