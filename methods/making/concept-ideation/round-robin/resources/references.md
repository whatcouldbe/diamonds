## Templates

### Standard Template (`LUMA_Round_Robin_11x17.pdf`)
The default paper template. An 11x17 sheet folded into four sections:
1. **Challenge Statement** — written at the top, visible throughout
2. **Proposed Solution** — "Come up with an unconventional way to address the challenge"
3. **Why the Solution Will Fail** — "Review the proposed solution, and find a reason that it will fail. This is your chance to be the armchair critic!"
4. **Final Concept** — "Review the critique. Then, quickly generate an idea that resolves the issues raised."

Each section is separated by a fold line. Participants only see the section they're working on — the folds enforce both the sequence and the anonymity.

---

### Positioning Statement Template (`Round_Robin_PositioningStatement_template.pdf`)
Applies the Round Robin mechanic to developing a positioning statement. The generate pass uses a structured format: Who / What / For Whom / What Need / Against Whom or What / What's Different / So + Capabilities. The critique and resolve passes mirror the same structure, so each field is addressed individually. Useful when the goal is collaboratively authored organizational language rather than a product concept.

---

### Alternate Worlds Template (`Round_Robin_Alternate_Worlds_Template_11x17.pdf`)
Combines Round Robin with an Alternate Worlds creative constraint. Rather than starting from a blank generate pass, participants are given an unrelated "Alternate Company" and asked to borrow its brand attributes and combine them with enabling technologies to produce an unexpected concept. The generate pass expands into five steps:
1. **Brand Characteristics** — list six things the alternate company is known for
2. **Enabling Technologies** — list six current or emerging technologies in the relevant space
3. **Written Proposal** — use starred items from above to describe the concept
4. **Pictorial Proposal** — draw the concept
5. **Name & Tagline** — name the product/service and write a tagline

The critique and resolve passes then follow the standard Round Robin structure. Use when teams are stuck in familiar solution patterns and need a forcing function to think differently.
