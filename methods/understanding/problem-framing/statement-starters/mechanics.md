# Statement Starters — Mechanics

A method for converting research insights into "How Might We" design challenge questions that set up expansive, ambitious ideation.
**Phase:** Understanding | **Category:** Problem Framing

---

## Purpose Statements

Use these in the introduction — lead with why, not what:

- Turn what we've learned about the problem into a question that opens up the biggest possible range of solutions
- Make sure we're inviting bold thinking — not anchoring ideation to an obvious or incremental answer
- Bridge the research we've done with the creative work that comes next
- Give ideation a sharp, human-centered question to respond to

---

## Inputs

- **Research insights** — from Affinity Clustering, interviewing, or other discovery work. The stronger the insight, the more generative the statement.
- **Team members** — the people who did the research; they carry the understanding of what was learned
- **Sticky notes or digital board** — one statement per note
- **Markers**
- **Dot stickers** — for voting at the end

---

## Outputs

- A set of How Might We statements grounded in research
- A prioritized working question — the statement chosen as the basis for ideation
- Optional variations at different levels of ambition, held in reserve

---

## Process

### Introduction

| Step | What to say |
|---|---|
| Frame the need | We've done the work to understand the problem. Now we need to turn that understanding into a question that will drive what we do next. |
| Name the risk | How we frame the challenge determines the range of ideas we can generate. A poorly framed question produces predictable answers. A well-framed one opens up new territory. |
| Name the outcome | We'll walk away with a sharp, human-centered question — one that's bold enough to invite unexpected thinking, specific enough to give ideation direction. |
| Set the tone | We'll draft, improve, and then choose. There's no single right answer — we're looking for the framing that opens the most interesting space. |

### Instructions

| Step | What to say |
|---|---|
| Name the structure | A good How Might We statement has three parts: a strong verb, the context, and the people who will benefit. All three do work. |
| Explain the verb | This is the most important word. Avoid "improve," "enhance," "support" — they describe a direction without committing to a change. Use: eliminate, transform, ensure, make, reimagine, delight. |
| Explain context | The where, when, or what. Specific enough to give the question direction — not so specific it prescribes the answer. |
| Explain people | Name who benefits. This keeps the question human-centered and prevents the answer from drifting toward the system rather than the person. |
| Set up generation | We'll draft individually first, then share. For each insight from our research, try completing: "How might we..." |

### Execution

| Step | What to do / say |
|---|---|
| Individual drafting | Work from insights. For each problem area or pattern, draft one or more HMW statements — one per sticky. 5–10 minutes, individually. |
| Check for failure modes | Before sharing: Does the statement embed a solution? Is it a compound question? Is it too narrow (a metric) or too broad (nothing to act on)? |
| Make them provocative | Apply the five techniques where statements feel flat: metaphor, strong emotions, absolutes, action verbs, -est over -er. |
| Share and cluster | Present statements to the group. Cluster similar framings together. Discuss differences in level and ambition — where are we aiming? |

### End

| Step | What to do |
|---|---|
| Generate variations | Take the best statements and draft alternates at different levels. Which version opens the most interesting space? |
| Vote | Give each person dot stickers. Vote for the statement you most want to explore. Allow brief discussion before dots go down — but don't let discussion replace the vote. |
| Name the chosen statement | Read it aloud. Confirm it as the working question for ideation. Write it large where the group can see it. |
| Acknowledge what's held | Note any strong statements that weren't chosen — they're not discarded, just set aside. They may become the basis for a second workshop. |

### Next Steps

| Step | What to do |
|---|---|
| Carry it into ideation | The chosen HMW statement is the prompt for the next activity. Every idea generated should be a response to it. |
| Keep the variations | Document alternate statements — they're often useful later, when the team wants to explore a different angle on the same problem area. |
| Connect back to research | Be ready to trace any statement back to its source insight. If you can't, reconsider whether it's grounded. |

---

## Stuff

- **Time:** 30–45 minutes for a group session; 15–20 minutes solo
- **Sticky notes** — one statement per note
- **Markers**
- **Dot stickers** — for voting; 3–5 dots per person
- **Wall space or digital board** — for displaying and clustering statements
- **Research insights** — printed, displayed, or accessible; the source material for the session
