# Abstraction Laddering — Mechanics

A structured method for aligning a team on the right level of abstraction for a challenge before investing in solutions.
**Phase:** Understanding | **Category:** Problem Framing

---

## Purpose Statements

Use these in the introduction — lead with why, not what:

- Align the team on what we're actually trying to accomplish before we start solving
- Make sure we're working at the right altitude — specific enough to act on, broad enough to find the best solution
- Surface the real goals behind the directive — and the range of approaches that might serve them
- Prevent "great landing, wrong airport" — committing to the wrong thing before we've checked our direction

---

## Inputs

- **A directive** — the starting opportunity, initiative, or brief ("We need to...")
- **Team members** — the people who will work on the challenge, with relevant perspective
- **Optional: Sponsor or project owner** — their WHYs often reveal unstated goals
- **Yellow sticky notes** — for WHYs (goals and benefits)
- **Purple or blue sticky notes** — for HOWs (approaches and tactics)
- **Markers**
- **Ladder template** — pre-drawn with directive in the center, space above for WHYs, space below for HOWs (Miro or printed)

---

## Outputs

- A mapped ladder showing WHYs (goals/benefits) above the directive and HOWs (approaches/tactics) below, clustered and labeled
- A chosen framing: the statement the team agrees best represents the real challenge
- Shared vocabulary for goals and approaches to carry into the project

---

## Process

### Introduction

| Step | What to say |
|---|---|
| Frame the need | We've been given a directive, and before we start working on it, we want to make sure we're all pointed at the same thing |
| Name the risk | It's easy to start solving the wrong problem — or the right problem at the wrong level. This gives us time to align before we invest. |
| Name the outcome | We'll walk away with a clear framing of the challenge — one we've all chosen together — and initial vocabulary for the goals and approaches |
| Set the tone | We'll generate individually first, then share. There are no wrong answers in the generation phase — we're exploring, not deciding. The decision comes at the end. |

### Instructions

| Step | What to say |
|---|---|
| Name it accessibly | Don't lead with "abstraction laddering" unless the Luma name is relevant. Say: *"We're going to explore the whys and hows of this challenge."* |
| Show the ladder | The directive goes in the center. Above it are WHYs — goals and benefits. Below it are HOWs — approaches and tactics. |
| Explain the starters | For WHYs, start with "In order to..." or "So that..." For HOWs, start with "Enabled by..." or "By considering..." |
| Explain why starters matter | The starters hold you at the right level. "In order to..." keeps WHYs as outcomes, not activities. "Enabled by..." keeps HOWs as approaches, not specific solutions. |
| Anchor the HOWs | HOWs always respond to the original directive — not to a higher-order WHY. If someone wants to explore the HOWs of a WHY, that's a separate ladder. |
| Set up individual generation | We'll generate individually and silently first — one idea per sticky. Then we'll share and build the ladder together. |

### Execution

| Step | What to do / say |
|---|---|
| WHY generation | *"Why are we doing this?"* — 5–10 min silent individual generation, yellow stickies, "In order to..." / "So that..." starters |
| HOW generation | *"How might we do this?"* — 5–10 min silent individual generation, purple/blue stickies, "Enabled by..." / "By considering..." starters |
| Share and cluster | One person describes and places their item. Others place similar items nearby. Repeat until all stickies are placed. Rearrange as groupings emerge. Label the clusters. |

### End

| Step | What to do |
|---|---|
| Level-selection discussion | *"Which of these framings is most useful for what we need to do next?"* — too high = not actionable (and disconnected from organisational strategy); too low = too constraining given time and resource; right altitude = clear direction with room for the best solution |
| Choose the mechanic | Use discussion when the ladder is clear and the group is small. Use voting — dot votes on clusters or candidate framings — when there's more content or more participants, or when you want to surface genuine disagreement rather than let it hide. |
| Name the outcome explicitly | Three things can happen: (1) reframe upward — a WHY is the better challenge; (2) reframe downward — a HOW is the right scope given constraints; (3) confirm and contextualise — the original framing was right, and the team now has the context and vocabulary to back it up. All three are valid. Name whichever one happened. |
| Acknowledge the surprises | Note anything that shifted — a goal that wasn't on anyone's radar, a framing that turned out to be more useful than the original directive |
| Consider visual alignment | If HOWs roughly correspond to WHYs above them, organise them into columns before closing. It creates a readable path from tactic through directive to benefit and helps the team see the full picture. |

### Next Steps

| Step | What to do |
|---|---|
| Carry the vocabulary forward | The chosen framing becomes the working definition of the challenge. Use it to scope research, brief the team, and evaluate solutions. |
| Consider converting to HMW | The chosen framing often translates directly into a How Might We statement. Try it: "How might we [chosen framing]?" |
| Document the ladder | Keep the full ladder — the WHYs become implicit success criteria; the HOWs become initial hypotheses to explore or test |
| If sponsor was present | Confirm alignment with them on the chosen framing before the team moves forward |

---

## Stuff

- **Time:** 45–60 minutes for a group session; 15–20 minutes solo
- **Yellow sticky notes** — WHYs (goals and benefits)
- **Purple or blue sticky notes** — HOWs (approaches and tactics)
- **Markers**
- **Ladder template** — Miro board or printed template, with directive pre-placed in the center
- **Timer** — for individual generation rounds
