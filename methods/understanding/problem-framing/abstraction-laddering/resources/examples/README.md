## Real Example — Bankeo Certification Pathways

**Context:** A team at Bankeo was asked to align on a new certification program. Three participants generated WHYs and HOWs individually, then built a shared ladder together.

**Directive:** Align upon a new set of certification pathways and revised curriculum to support desired capability.

---

### WHYs

**Level 1 — Immediate benefits:**
- So that the program is recognized as a Bankeo program (not a vendor program)
- Reinvigorate and reenergize the program and the community of practice
- Draw on other people and practices that have enabled outcomes
- Tie success measures to outcomes that are relevant to Bankeo roles and the business
- Reduce the amount of administration certification currently requires

**Level 2 — Higher-order benefits:**
- Make the benefits of certification valuable and visible
- Develop actual capability and capacity to affect more people and projects across Bankeo
- Focus on application and enablement over "training"
- Equip Bankeo with up-to-date and in-demand skills
- Make it possible to engage at every level of capability

**Level 3 — Highest-order goals:**
- Bankeo creates their own standard (recognized externally)
- Create a culture of continuous learning
- Reduce skill gaps

---

### HOWs

**Level 1 — Approaches:**
- Draw upon lessons from other certification programs and transitions
- Showcase a broad range of applications
- Establish agreed upon definitions of roles
- Mine existing feedback on the current pathways
- Co-create pathways with Bankeo stakeholders
- Create evidence-based criteria for achieving certification
- Create a Bankeo certification
- Solutions specific to one's needs, which may be role-based and situation-based
- Include methods beyond the current system while maintaining a common language
- Allow customers to use Bankeo content

**Level 2 — Tactics (selected branches):**
- By Bankeo offering their own internal certification
- Self certification
- Peer-to-peer certification
- Certify internally
- Certify with other external organizations
- Certification is based on evidence
- Certification based upon specific roles

---

### What This Example Shows

**The directive was already well-framed** — specific enough to be actionable, but the WHY ladder revealed meaningful goals that weren't explicit in the brief: building Bankeo's own external-facing standard, creating a culture of continuous learning, and reducing skill gaps. These became implicit success criteria.

**The HOW ladder surfaced creative approaches** — particularly the certification model options (self-certification, peer-to-peer, internal certification). The team had likely been assuming a traditional top-down certification structure; the ladder opened up alternatives.

**The individual generation step produced real diversity** — participants Claudia, Justin, and Steven brought noticeably different perspectives in their individual workspaces before the shared ladder was built.

## Illustrative Example — Employee Onboarding

**Directive:** We need to improve the new employee onboarding experience.

**WHYs (selected):**
- Level 1: In order to reduce time-to-productivity for new hires
- Level 2: So that teams can operate at full capacity sooner after a hire
- Level 3: In order to build an organization that can grow without losing momentum

**HOWs (selected):**
- Level 1: Enabled by a more structured first-90-days framework
- Level 2: By creating role-specific onboarding tracks rather than a one-size-fits-all program
- Level 3: By embedding new hires in real project work from week one

**Chosen framing:** Reduce time-to-productivity for new hires by making the onboarding experience more role-specific and hands-on.

**What shifted:** The team had been focused on improving the onboarding materials (a HOW). The WHY ladder revealed that the real goal was time-to-productivity — and that better materials alone wouldn't get there. The chosen framing opened up solutions (mentorship, early project assignment, manager check-ins) that the original directive had closed off.
