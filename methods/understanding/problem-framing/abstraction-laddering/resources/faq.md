# Abstraction Laddering — FAQ

Questions that come up regularly when teams use or learn this method. Grows over time.

---

**How many levels should we ladder up or down?**
Two to three levels in each direction is usually enough for a working session. You can keep going, but the higher you go, the more abstract and less actionable the goals become. The point isn't to reach the highest possible WHY — it's to find the right altitude. Stop when you reach a level that's genuinely useful for framing the work.

**What if our WHYs all end up sounding the same?**
This sometimes means the directive is already framed at a reasonably high level — there isn't much room to go higher before reaching something too abstract to act on. It can also mean the team is anchored to the same mental model. Try prompting for unusual or unexpected benefits: "What's a WHY that nobody would expect?" or "What's a goal that might surprise your sponsor?"

**What if we can't agree on the right level?**
The disagreement is the insight. If two people want to work at different altitudes, that means they have different assumptions about what the project is for. Surface that directly: "It sounds like we have two different framings here — let's talk about which one better reflects what we're actually being asked to do." Sometimes the right answer is to go back to the sponsor and check.

**Can we use this method after a project has already started?**
Yes. It's most powerful at the beginning, but it's also useful when a team suspects they've drifted from the original intent — or when new information suggests the framing needs to shift. Running abstraction laddering mid-project is a legitimate way to recalibrate.

**What's the difference between WHYs and success metrics?**
WHYs are goals and benefits — qualitative statements about what the project should make possible. Success metrics are the measurable indicators of whether you've achieved those goals. The WHYs come first; the metrics come from them. The ladder surfaces the goals; measurement design turns them into something trackable.

**What do we do if the chosen framing is very different from the original directive?**
Take it back to the sponsor. This is a feature, not a bug — you've discovered that the team's understanding of the challenge is different from (or deeper than) what was originally handed down. That's a critical alignment conversation to have before the team invests further. Come with the ladder as context, not just a different conclusion.

**Is this the same as asking "five whys"?**
Related but different. Five whys is a root cause analysis tool — it's used to find the underlying cause of a problem, and it moves in one direction (up). Abstraction laddering goes both directions (goals above, approaches below), starts with a directive rather than a problem, and ends with a team choosing a framing level rather than identifying a root cause. The questioning instinct is similar; the purpose and structure are different.
