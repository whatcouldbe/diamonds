# Abstraction Laddering — References

External reading, related frameworks, and resources for going deeper. Grows over time.

---

## Origin

Abstraction laddering was developed by **Kees Dorst and Nigel Cross** as part of their research into design cognition. It draws on earlier work in problem framing and the recognition that how a problem is stated fundamentally shapes what solutions are possible.

The method has been widely adopted in design thinking and innovation contexts, where it's often called **Why/How Laddering** or **How/Why Laddering** depending on the practitioner.

---

## Related Frameworks

**Five Whys** — A root cause analysis tool from lean manufacturing (Toyota Production System). Asks "why" repeatedly to find the underlying cause of a problem. Related in spirit — both use why-questioning to move past surface-level framing — but different in purpose. Five Whys diagnoses causes; abstraction laddering finds the right framing level.

**Means-End Analysis** — A problem-solving approach from cognitive psychology that maps the relationship between goals (ends) and actions (means). Abstraction laddering draws on similar logic — WHYs map to ends, HOWs map to means.

**HMW (How Might We)** — A reframing technique developed at Procter & Gamble and popularized through IDEO and Stanford d.school. Often used after abstraction laddering to convert a chosen framing into a design challenge.
