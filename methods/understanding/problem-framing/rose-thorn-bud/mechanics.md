# Rose, Thorn, Bud — Mechanics

A codification method for organizing observations and feedback into three categories: what's working (rose), what isn't (thorn), and what has potential (bud).
**Phase:** Understanding | **Category:** Problem Framing

---

## Purpose Statements

Use these in the introduction — lead with why, not what:

- Convert observations and notes into a consistent, shareable format
- Give everyone a voice and ensure multiple perspectives are captured
- Make patterns in feedback or research data visible
- Prepare data for synthesis and analysis
- Create a visual heat map of what's working, what's challenging, and where the opportunities are

---

## Inputs

- A body of data to codify: research notes, interview observations, workshop feedback, personal reflections
- Sticky notes in three colors: pink (rose), blue (thorn), green (bud)
- Participants who have first-hand experience of the thing being reviewed — or notes from those who do

---

## Outputs

- Color-coded items on a board — one observation per note
- A visual heat map showing the distribution of positive, negative, and potential observations
- A structured dataset ready for affinity clustering or other synthesis

---

## Process

### Introduction

| Step | What to say |
|---|---|
| Frame the need | We've got a body of observations / feedback / research data and we want to make sense of it |
| Name the benefit | This activity gives everyone a voice and converts our data into a consistent format we can all work with |
| Name the outcome | We'll end with a color-coded set of items that shows us what's working, what isn't, and where the opportunities are |
| Introduce the colors | Pink is a rose — something positive, something working well. Blue is a thorn — something challenging or not working. Green is a bud — something with potential, an opportunity or emerging idea. |

### Instructions

| Step | What to say |
|---|---|
| One item per note | Each observation, quote, or thought gets its own sticky note — one item, one note |
| Write enough | Write enough that someone who wasn't there could understand it. A word or two isn't enough — give it some context. |
| No interpretation | Record what you saw or heard, not what you think it means. Analysis comes later. |
| Aim for spread | Try to generate items across all three colors. Two or three per color is a good starting point if time is limited. If one color dominates, that's fine — it reflects reality. |
| Invite questions | Any questions before we get started? |

### Execution

| Step | What to do |
|---|---|
| Individual generation | Participants write their observations on sticky notes in the appropriate color, one per note |
| Place on board | Items go on the board — grouped by color initially, or placed in open space ready for clustering |
| Read aloud | In a group setting, have participants read their items aloud as they place them. This builds shared understanding and surfaces items that need clarification. |
| Note repetition | If the same observation appears across multiple people, flag it — repetition signals a pattern |

### End

| Step | What to do |
|---|---|
| Read the heat map | Step back and look at the color distribution. What's the ratio of roses to thorns to buds? Where is the weight of the data? |
| Surface patterns | Are there clusters of similar items, even before formal clustering? Name what you're seeing. |
| Flag divergence | If the same observation appears as different colors for different people, name it — that divergence is a finding |

### Next Steps

| Step | What to do |
|---|---|
| Move to clustering | Take the color-coded items into an affinity clustering session to identify themes and insights |
| Document | Photograph or export the board before moving items |
| Share | Share the raw output with team members who weren't present |

---

## Stuff

- **Time:** ~10 minutes as a workshop activity (2–3 minutes per color); variable for research codification
- **Target dataset:** 20–30 items total is a useful volume for affinity clustering
- **Sticky notes:** Three colors — pink (rose), blue (thorn), green (bud). Consistent color use throughout.
- **Board:** Physical wall or digital workspace (Miro, FigJam). No special setup required — start with blank space.
- **Visual:** The RTB color guide (`resources/visuals/rtb-color-guide.png`) is useful to show during introduction — it displays the three categories alongside their alternative names
