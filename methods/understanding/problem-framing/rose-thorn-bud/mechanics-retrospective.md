# Rose, Thorn, Bud — Retrospective Mechanics

Rose, Thorn, Bud applied to a team retrospective: a structured approach for moving a team from individual reflection to shared insight to committed action.
**Phase:** Understanding | **Category:** Problem Framing | **Context:** Team Retrospective

---

## Purpose Statements

Use these in the opening — connect the activity to why the team is here:

- Create the conditions for honest reflection — this is a learning session, not a debrief or a blame session
- Give every voice equal weight before group dynamics take over
- Surface patterns the team can actually act on, not just vent about
- Leave with specific commitments, not good intentions

---

## Inputs

- A defined scope: the sprint, quarter, project, or period being reviewed — named explicitly before the session starts
- Team members with direct experience of the work being reviewed
- A shared board with three columns: Rose (what went well), Thorn (what went wrong), Bud (what can we try to do better)
- 40–90 minutes, scaled to team size and scope

---

## Outputs

- Color-coded observations across three categories, one per sticky note
- Clustered themes with dot-voted priorities
- 2–3 committed actions, each with a clear owner and a specific deadline

---

## Process

### Open the Session

| Step | What to say or do |
|---|---|
| Set the frame | "We're here to learn from [scope], not assign blame. Everything we discuss is in service of making the next [sprint / quarter / project] better." |
| Name the three commitments | Psychological safety (everyone speaks honestly), shared understanding (we move from individual input to collective insight), follow-through (we leave with real commitments). |
| Introduce the three prompts | "We're using Rose, Thorn, Bud to structure our reflection. Rose: what went well. Thorn: what went wrong. Bud: what can we try to do better." |

### Gather Data

| Step | What to say or do |
|---|---|
| Generate individually first | Give 5–10 minutes of silent writing before any discussion. "Take the time to write — we'll talk after." |
| One item per note | "One observation per sticky. Write enough that someone who wasn't on this team could understand it." |
| Hold the boundary | No discussion during generation. Group sense-making comes next — protect the silence. |
| Place notes on the board | Each person adds their stickies to the appropriate column. In a digital session, everyone drops notes simultaneously before anyone reads. |

### Understand Insights

| Step | What to say or do |
|---|---|
| Read the board first | Before touching anything, pause: "What do we see?" Let the distribution land. |
| Cluster by theme | Move similar items together — across categories if needed. Name each cluster with a short label. |
| Dot vote | Give each participant 3–5 dot votes. "Put your dots on the themes that feel most important to address." |
| Name the priorities | The highest-voted clusters become the inputs to action. "These are what we're going to focus on." |

### Decide Actions

| Step | What to say or do |
|---|---|
| Work through the top clusters | Start with the highest-voted theme. "What would actually change if we addressed this?" |
| Make it specific | Every action needs three things: **What** will we do, **who** owns it, and **by when**. |
| Hold the limit | Stop at 2–3 actions. More than that and follow-through degrades. "Better to do two things well than commit to six and do none." |
| Read the actions back | Before closing, read each commitment aloud. Confirm the owner and deadline with the owner present. |

### Close

| Step | What to say or do |
|---|---|
| Acknowledge what was hard | Name anything that was honest or uncomfortable. Don't skip this — it reinforces safety for next time. |
| Set the expectation | "We'll open the next retro by reviewing these commitments." |
| Protect the data | Export or archive the board. The action log travels forward. |

---

## Facilitation Watchouts

- **Venting without moving forward** — If thorns overwhelm the board, name it: "We're seeing a lot here. Our job now is to figure out what we can actually change." Then move to clustering.
- **Buds that are really thorns** — Participants sometimes write criticisms as improvements. Don't correct it during generation — let it land in the bud column and sort it at clustering.
- **Actions without owners** — "The team will…" is not an action. Push until a specific person's name is attached.
- **Overcommitting** — Teams routinely commit to more than they can do. Hold the limit at 2–3 and name why.
- **Recurring issues** — If a theme appeared in a prior retro, name it directly: "This came up last time. What stopped us from addressing it?"

---

## Stuff

- **Time:** 40 minutes minimum (tight); 60–75 minutes is comfortable for a team of 4–6; 90 minutes for larger groups or broader scope
- **Dot votes:** 3–5 per person
- **Action limit:** 2–3 maximum
- **Board setup:** Three columns (Rose / Thorn / Bud) for data gathering; separate section for actions (What / Who / By when)
- **Colors:** Pink (rose), blue (thorn), green (bud) — consistent color use helps participants orient quickly
- **Scope framing:** Name the review period in the board header before the session starts. Ambiguity about what's being reviewed is the fastest way to lose the room.
