# Importance / Difficulty Matrix — Mechanics

A quad chart for plotting items by relative importance and difficulty.
**Phase:** Understanding | **Category:** Patterns & Priorities

---

## Purpose Statements

Use these in the introduction — lead with why, not what:

- Help prioritize items quickly
- Facilitate deliberation and discussion
- Resolve differing opinions
- Ensure we align on the most impactful things to work on
- Develop a plan of action

---

## Inputs

- **8–12 pre-populated items** to prioritize — tasks, features, initiatives, research findings, or similar. Each item on its own sticky note or digital equivalent.
- **A group of people** familiar with the subject matter that need to decide what to focus upon
- **A definition of importance** relevant to this context (e.g., "Moves us closer to our objective. Creates more value for users or the business.")
- **A definition of difficulty** relevant to this context (e.g., "Requires more time, budget, or organizational change. Demands capabilities or expertise we don't currently have.")

---

## Outputs

- Items characterized as: quick wins (high importance, low difficulty), high-value/strategic (high importance, high difficulty), fill-ins (low importance, low difficulty), and luxuries (low importance, high difficulty)
- A suggestion where to start — with items that yield great impact at low cost
- An agreed-upon set of priorities
- The beginning of an action plan

---

## Process

### Introduction

| Step | What to say |
|---|---|
| Frame the need | We need to quickly prioritize [these things] |
| Name the benefit | This activity will help us facilitate a good discussion to uncover and resolve differing opinions |
| Name the outcome | This will ensure we align on the most impactful things to work on |
| Connect to action | And help us develop a plan of action |

### Instructions

| Step | What to say |
|---|---|
| Explain importance | Here's how this is going to work — we'll start with importance, which for this project means [definition] |
| Explain the process | Someone will place the first item in the middle of the x-axis and then we'll discuss every item by relative importance until we have all items in a single line from left to right |
| Explain difficulty | Once we have importance complete, we'll do the same for difficulty, which for this project means [definition] |
| Invite questions | Any questions before we get started? |

### Execution

| Step | What to do |
|---|---|
| Start importance | Place any single item in the center of the x-axis to start discussing importance |
| Rank importance | Discuss each remaining item one by one. Ask: "Is this more or less important than items already placed?" Position accordingly — left being less important, right being more important. Refer to importance criteria as needed. Once all items are placed, each with its own unique position, spread them evenly across the full width of the x-axis. |
| Start difficulty | Maintaining its left-to-right position, move one item to the center of the y-axis to start discussing difficulty |
| Rank difficulty | Maintaining all left-to-right positions, discuss each remaining item one by one. Ask: "Is this more or less difficult than items already placed?" Move items up or down accordingly. Use the agreed criteria to guide discussions. Once all items are placed, each with its own unique position, spread them evenly across the full height of the y-axis. |

### End

| Step | What to do |
|---|---|
| Reveal quadrants | Add horizontal and vertical center lines to the working space, making four equal quadrants |
| Label quadrants | Bottom right = "High ROI" (returns on investment). Bottom left = "Quick wins" (need less planning). Top right = "Strategic" (need careful planning). Top left = "Luxury" or "Low ROI" (not now). |
| Discuss patterns | Discuss the completed matrix and any patterns you see. Are there more items than expected in one quadrant? What connections are there between items? Where should we start? |
| Draft roadmap | Which High ROI items should be worked on first? Which Quick Wins will be helpful to work in? Which Strategic initiatives need project planning? Which Luxury items are you explicitly deferring? Assign owners to action items. |

### Next Steps

| Step | What to do |
|---|---|
| Set timeframes | Set timeframes for assigned action items |
| Record | Record the output and decisions made in a format that can be shared with everyone who needs it |
| Share | Share the matrix with stakeholders who weren't present |
| Refer back | Refer back to the agreed session output as work progresses |

---

## Stuff

- **Time:** 45–60 minutes for a typical session
- **Workspace:** A 2×2 grid with Importance on the x-axis and Difficulty on the y-axis — digital (Miro/FigJam) or physical (whiteboard). Keep the y-axis hidden until needed.
- **Items:** 8–12 pre-populated items to prioritize — tasks, features, initiatives, or similar. Each item on its own sticky note or digital equivalent.
- **Criteria:** Agreed definitions of "importance" and "difficulty" relevant to your context. These guide discussion and keep decisions consistent.
- **Participants:** 4–8 people who understand the work being prioritized and are decision-makers or can influence decisions. If running with more than 8 participants, split into two parallel groups — each with their own workspace.
