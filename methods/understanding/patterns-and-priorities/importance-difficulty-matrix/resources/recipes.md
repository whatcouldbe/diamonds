# Importance / Difficulty Matrix — Recipes

How the I/D Matrix combines with other methods. Recipes describe natural sequences — what typically comes before, what typically follows, and useful combinations for specific situations.

---

## The Core Recipe: Research to Road Map

The most common sequence — from raw research findings to a prioritized action plan:

```
Research (interviews, surveys, observation)
    ↓
Affinity Clustering — synthesize findings into themes
    ↓
Importance / Difficulty Matrix — prioritize the themes
    ↓
Sequenced road map or business cases
```

The matrix is the bridge between synthesis and action. Without it, teams often get stuck — they have insights but no agreed sequence for acting on them.

---

## Recipe: Narrowing Before the Matrix

When you have more than 10 items, use simpler methods to narrow first:

```
Brainstorm / Concept Ideation — generate a large set of ideas or initiatives
    ↓
Bullseye Diagramming — rank by importance to narrow to 8–10
    ↓
Importance / Difficulty Matrix — add the difficulty dimension for trade-off discussion
```

Bullseye is one-dimensional (importance only). The matrix adds the difficulty axis, enabling genuine trade-off conversation. Use Bullseye to cut the list; use the matrix to make the hard calls.

---

## Recipe: Prioritizing Critique Feedback

After running a critique session that produces a large set of feedback items:

```
Critique — collect feedback (strengths, challenges, suggestions)
    ↓
Affinity Clustering — group similar feedback into themes
    ↓
Importance / Difficulty Matrix — decide which feedback to act on first
    ↓
Iterate on the artifact
    ↓
Critique again
```

Not all feedback deserves equal investment. The matrix makes the prioritization explicit and gives the team a shared basis for deciding what to address.

---

## Recipe: Aligning a Mixed Stakeholder Group

When you need to align people with different areas of expertise and perspective:

```
Prepare items in advance (from research, discovery, or team input)
    ↓
Importance / Difficulty Matrix — with a group that includes both people who understand importance AND people who understand difficulty
    ↓
Critique the matrix output — "Does this feel right? What might we be missing?"
    ↓
Road map with buy-in from all participants
```

The power of the matrix is that it forces people with different lenses to engage with each other's perspective. Someone who understands implementation difficulty has to engage with strategic importance, and vice versa.

---

## Recipe: Retrospective Check

At the end of a cycle, use the matrix to evaluate whether priorities were actually addressed:

```
Previous I/D Matrix output (what we said we'd do)
    ↓
Retrospective — what did we actually do? What did we defer?
    ↓
Importance / Difficulty Matrix — re-run with updated context and new items
    ↓
Adjusted road map
```

This creates a feedback loop — the matrix becomes a living prioritization tool, not a one-time artifact.

---

## Related Methods

| Method | Relationship |
|---|---|
| **Affinity Clustering** | Most common precursor — cluster insights or ideas, then prioritize the clusters |
| **Bullseye Diagramming** | Simpler precursor when you have too many items — one-dimensional importance ranking |
| **Critique** | Often follows the matrix to evaluate a proposed road map, or feeds the matrix with prioritized feedback |
| **Visualize the Vote (dot voting)** | Simpler alternative — captures preference but not trade-offs. Use when time is very short or the decision is low-stakes |
| **Eisenhower Matrix** | Related structure with a different second dimension (urgency instead of difficulty) — more suited to personal task management than strategic road mapping |
| **Business Case Development** | Natural follow-on — each item in the strategic quadrant is a potential business case to make |
