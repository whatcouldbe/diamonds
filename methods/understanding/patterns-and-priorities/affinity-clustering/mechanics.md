# Affinity Clustering — Mechanics

A synthesis method that organizes individual data items into clusters by similarity to surface themes and insights.
**Phase:** Understanding | **Category:** Patterns & Priorities

---

## Purpose Statements

Use these in the introduction — lead with why, not what:

- Make sense of a large body of research data
- Surface themes and patterns that aren't obvious from individual items
- Identify what the data is collectively telling us
- Build shared understanding of the research across the team
- Generate insight seeds ready for prioritization or design framing

---

## Inputs

- **Individual data items** on sticky notes — typically from user research (interviews, contextual inquiry, observation), often pre-codified using Rose, Thorn, Bud
- **A blank workspace** — no pre-labeled sections or buckets; themes emerge from the data
- **2–4 participants** familiar with the data; works individually or asynchronously

---

## Outputs

- A clustered board with items organized into thematic groups
- Cluster titles — full sentences naming each insight (not category labels)
- A heat map of sentiment distribution across clusters (when RTB color coding is present)
- "What did we learn about this cluster and why is that important?" statements for priority clusters
- Insight seeds ready for How Might We framing or prioritization

---

## Process

### Introduction

| Step | What to say |
|---|---|
| Frame the need | We've gathered data from [research activity] and we want to understand what it's telling us |
| Name the benefit | This activity will help us surface the themes and patterns hidden across all these individual items |
| Name the outcome | We'll end with a set of clusters, each with a title that names an insight — ready for prioritization or design framing |
| Set the ground rule | We start with a blank area. We let the themes emerge from the data — we don't decide in advance what the categories will be |

### Instructions

| Step | What to say |
|---|---|
| Explain the criterion | We're grouping items by affinity — by what they have in common in terms of meaning. Not by sentiment, not by who said them, not by topic bucket. |
| Explain the process | One person describes and places an item. Others place similar items nearby. We repeat until everything is placed. Then we discuss, rearrange, and label. |
| Explain the colors | If using RTB: the colors show what's working (rose/pink), what's challenging (thorn/blue), and what has potential (bud/green). A cluster can have all three — that's expected. |
| Invite questions | Any questions before we get started? |

### Execution

| Step | What to do |
|---|---|
| Start | One person picks an item, reads it aloud, places it on the blank board |
| Build | Ask: "Who has something that's a bit like that?" Others place similar items nearby, reading each one aloud |
| Repeat | When no more items have affinity with the current cluster, pick a different item and start again. Rotate who places items. |
| Rearrange | Once all items are placed, discuss and adjust. Move items, split large clusters, combine near-identical ones. |

### End

| Step | What to do |
|---|---|
| Label clusters | Work through each cluster and give it a title — a full sentence, ideally led with a verb. Apply the three-part test: what is the title? what is the insight? what is the action? |
| Interrogate large clusters | Any cluster with more than ~8 items — ask: "Is this one theme or two?" Split if needed. |
| Read the heat map | If using RTB: identify clusters with high proportions of thorns and buds — these are often where the design work happens |
| Articulate the insight | For priority clusters: "What did we learn about this cluster, and why is that important?" |

### Next Steps

| Step | What to do |
|---|---|
| Connect to prioritization | Take cluster titles into Importance/Difficulty Matrix or Bullseye Diagramming to decide where to focus |
| Connect to framing | Strong cluster titles convert directly to How Might We statements — each is a potential design brief |
| Document | Record the board in a format that can be shared. Photograph or export. |
| Share | Share with stakeholders or team members who weren't present |

---

## Stuff

- **Time:** ~45 minutes; set a timer as a forcing function
- **People:** 2–4 working in real time; works individually or asynchronously
- **Workspace:** A blank board — physical (wall, table) or digital (Miro, FigJam). No pre-labeled sections.
- **Data:** Individual items on sticky notes, one item per note. Color-coded if RTB has been used (pink/rose, blue/thorn, green/bud).
- **Optional:** "What did we learn about this cluster and why is that important?" prompt cards for labeling priority clusters
