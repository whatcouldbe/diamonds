# The Key Questions

This is a process framework that guides teams through innovation work using questions as checkpoints. It's tool-agnostic - you can use HCD methods, Lean, strategic frameworks, or other approaches to answer each question. The key principle: **If you can answer the question with validated knowledge, move forward. If not, iterate to validate.**

Each question is a key. Answering it with validated knowledge unlocks the next one. You cannot move forward on assumption alone.

## Core Principle

Each question is a validation gate. You cannot move forward until you can answer it with validated knowledge — not assumption, not confidence, not consensus. Validated knowledge means you've tested it with real people and have evidence.

At every gate, there are three possible outcomes:
- **Move forward** — you've validated the answer. Proceed.
- **Iterate** — you haven't validated it yet. Keep working until you can.
- **Stop** — you've validated that the answer is "no." There's no real need, no viable problem, no customer for this solution. Stopping is the right call. Moving forward anyway is how teams build the wrong thing.

The questions are process-agnostic. They apply regardless of methodology — Double Diamond, Agile, Lean, or anything else. Any process is just one way to answer these questions. The questions themselves are the universal checkpoints. What matters is whether you can answer them with evidence, not which tools you used to get there.

## The Key Questions Framework

### **Pre-Discovery: Strategic Context**

Before you start the double diamond, establish why you're doing this work.

#### **Question 1: What goals do we need to achieve, and how will we know we've achieved them?**

**Team's Core Task:** Set strategic goals and communicate so that they can be proposed and evaluated against objectives.

**Outcomes:**
- Define business, org, department, metrics, or milestones
- Establish the strategic context for the work
- Align team efforts that support organizational goals

**Leader's Core Tasks:** Set and communicate strategic goals so that your team can comprehend, can propose ideas to deliver upon stated objectives during strategic planning.

**Questions leaders can ask to validate:**
- What do we want to achieve?
- How will we measure that we've achieved it?
- How does this work support achieving progress?

**Can you move on?** When you have clear, measurable goals that the team understands and agrees on.

---

#### **Question 2: What should we be working on to achieve our goals?**

**Action: Set your project up for success**

**Team's Core Task:** Align on a plan as a team that prioritizes resources against proposals with the highest potential impact to achieve strategic goals.

**Outcomes:**
- Team alignment upon a clearly prioritized plan to capture approach and resources
- Signoff from stakeholders and agreement with team
- Identify actions that drive efforts
- Identify a multi-disciplinary team with local representation beyond immediate team

**Leader's Core Tasks:** Confirm we have realistic plans that clearly prioritize and allocate resources against the highest impact proposals to achieve our strategic goals.

**Questions leaders can ask to validate:**
- What early or existing insights do we have that can inform what we do?
- What's the plan to discover whether the solution will work for our customers?
- What potential impact will these approaches have on our goals?

**Can you move on?** When the team is aligned on priorities and has stakeholder buy-in to proceed.

---

### **First Diamond: Discover → Define**

Validate the problem space before jumping to solutions.

#### **Question 3: What are people actually trying to accomplish?**

**Action: DISCOVER what people need**

**Team's Core Task:** Discover whether we truly are addressing real customer partners' needs and identify what matters most to them.

**Outcomes:**
- Understand what motivates the people you are designing for
- Identify the needs, goals, and frustrations that represent real opportunity
- Know how people are currently trying to solve the problem on their own

**Leader's Core Tasks:** Agree whether to continue working on current plans or change them based on new evidence around what people actually need.

**Questions leaders can ask to validate:**
- What are people actually trying to accomplish?
- How are they currently trying to accomplish it?
- What workarounds or compensating behaviors have we observed?
- What key insights did we discover from spending time with real people?
- What did we learn that surprised us or challenged our assumptions?

**Can you move on?** When you've spent time with real customers, observed their actual behavior, and validated what matters to them. Not when you think you know - when you've validated with real people.

**This is a Discover question** - you need qualitative research (interviews, observation) to truly see and hear what people do and think.

---

#### **Question 4: What are the most impactful problems to solve?**

**Action: DEFINE the best opportunities**

**Team's Core Task:** Prioritize the most valuable problem(s) to solve and outline the opportunity space(s) for new ideas and concepts.

**Outcomes:**
- Verify the problem is worth solving
- Verify that this is a problem the organization should solve — that it is viable in the marketplace and technically feasible
- Build upon a foundation of evidence and data, not assumption
- Define a clear opportunity statement that connects customer needs to business goals

**Leader's Core Tasks:** Verify the most valuable problem(s) to solve have been defined before moving into solution development.

**Questions leaders can ask to validate:**
- What evidence do we have that this is the right problem?
- What is the actual problem, stated clearly?
- Have we connected this to what people are trying to accomplish?
- Have we uncovered any unmet needs we hadn't anticipated?
- Why is this the most valuable problem to solve right now?

**Can you move on?** When you've synthesized insights from Q3, identified patterns, and framed a validated opportunity tied to outcomes for both customers and the business. You should be able to articulate the problem clearly and explain why solving it matters.

**This is a Define question** - you need synthesis and sense-making to converge on what's most valuable.

---

### **Second Diamond: Develop → Deliver**

Validate which solution approach actually works.

#### **Question 5: How might we best solve the problem?**

**Action: DEVELOP innovative ideas**

**Team's Core Task:** Explore new ideas and create shareable concepts. Make improvements by iterating on customer feedback.

**Outcomes:**
- Search existing solutions that can solve the identified problem
- Consider emerging technology
- Generate a range of innovative ideas
- Make ideas and concepts tangible and shareable
- Iterate and develop ideas and concepts

**Leader's Core Tasks:** Encourage broad exploration of new concepts and rounds of improvement and iteration based on customer feedback.

**Questions leaders can ask to validate:**
- What stimulus are we using to inspire our thinking about the future?
- How will we engage customers to solve the customer's problem?
- Why is our concept better than what exists today?
- What other services might the customer be using in conjunction with our solution?
- Who else participated in the concept ideation?
- How widely were ideas cast?
- Show me what the future would look like if this concept is successful.

**Can you move on?** When you've explored multiple approaches (not just one), made concepts tangible enough to test, and gotten feedback that helps you understand which direction works best. Remember: there are many ways to deliver on an opportunity - testing reveals the best one.

**This is a Develop question** - you need ideation and prototyping methods to explore possibilities.

---

#### **Question 6: What can we build to test whether our solution works for people?**

**Action: DELIVER something impactful**

**Team's Core Task:** Test our ability to deliver the solution successfully to customers and validate market demand.

**Outcomes:**
- Ensure the concept is useful, usable and viable
- Test, iterate and refine the solution based on feedback
- Ensure the solution is feasible for an organization to develop
- Ensure the solution is desirable enough that customers pay for it
- Be open to killing ideas that don't work

**Leader's Core Tasks:** Verify we are able to deliver a solution successfully to customers that validates market demand.

**Questions leaders can ask to validate:**
- Which customers will be the most useful to validate our solution with?
- What evidence have we gathered that this works — and does it need to be broken down into component parts to test?
- How do we know this solution is better than what exists today?
- How are you capturing evidence that your solution is desirable and viable?
- How might we time-box testing?

**Can you move on?** When you've validated that your solution actually delivers the outcomes you intended - for customers AND for the business. Not when you've built something polished, but when you've tested that it works and delivers value.

**This is a Deliver question** - you need validation through testing, not assumption.

---

### **Post-Launch: Lifecycle**

Sustain, scale, or sunset based on validated performance.

#### **Question 7: What will we do to launch the validated solution?**

**Team's Core Task:** Implement the solution and acquire new customers.

**Outcomes:**
- Compliance with any relevant requirements
- Produced materials for marketing and sales activities
- Ensure infrastructure is in place for post-launch support and managing quality events
- Clarify the financing model
- Carry out a go-to-market scaling and handover

**Leader's Core Tasks:** Verify we are able to implement and support the solution successfully from awareness through adoption.

**Questions leaders can ask to validate:**
- How will we provide support?
- What will be the ideal customer acquisition?
- What dates on boarding look like?
- How will people become aware of the solution?
- What are the biggest implementation risks and how are we mitigating them?

**Can you move on?** When you have a go-to-market plan, required materials, and the validated solution is actually in customers' hands.

---

#### **Question 8: What can we do to accelerate growth?**

**Team's Core Task:** Scale the validated solution to more people to generate greater business outcomes.

**Outcomes:**
- Accelerate growth across reach (more people using it), revenue (greater return), and results (stronger outcomes)
- Identify the highest-impact opportunities for expansion — geographic, demographic, or capability-based
- Continuously track customer and solution health as scale increases

**Leader's Core Tasks:** Plan to scale the validated solution to more people to generate greater business outcomes.

**Questions leaders can ask to validate:**
- What is currently driving growth, and how do we amplify it?
- What could limit or slow growth, and how do we address it?
- How do we measure whether we're scaling effectively?
- What advantages do we have relative to alternatives in the market?
- What can we do to expand our user base while maintaining the value that made this work?

**Can you move on?** When you understand what's driving growth and have a plan to amplify it systematically.

---

#### **Question 9: How do we keep adding value and stay relevant?**

**Team's Core Task:** Continuously improve the solution so it remains valuable to customers and worth sustaining for the business.

**Outcomes:**
- Identify opportunities to improve based on ongoing customer and market feedback
- Optimize efficiency and reduce cost where possible without compromising value
- Monitor whether the solution continues to address the original problem effectively

**Leader's Core Tasks:** Ensure the solution continues to deliver value while managing costs and identifying when significant reinvestment or change is needed.

**Questions leaders can ask to validate:**
- Are customers still getting meaningful value from this solution?
- What feedback or signals suggest the solution needs to evolve?
- What would it take to keep this solution relevant for the next 1–2 years?
- Are we investing the right amount to sustain this?
- Is there a point at which we should consider stopping?

**Can you move on?** This is an ongoing question — there is no single "done." Continue monitoring and improving. When the investment required to stay relevant exceeds the value delivered, move to the next question.

**Note:** Sustain is not a terminal state. If ongoing effort reveals new unmet needs or shifting goals, it may be worth looping back to re-examine what should be worked on next.

---

#### **Question 10: Is this solution still worth the investment, or is it time to stop?**

**Team's Core Task:** Assess whether the solution continues to provide sufficient value to justify ongoing investment, and if not, manage a responsible wind-down.

**Outcomes:**
- Capture and transfer knowledge from this solution to inform future work
- Realign resources freed by stopping toward higher-value opportunities
- Ensure customers are supported through any transition

**Leader's Core Tasks:** Make a clear, evidence-based decision about whether to continue investing in or stop this solution.

**Questions leaders can ask to validate:**
- What evidence tells us whether this solution is still delivering value?
- What would it cost to keep this solution viable versus what it currently delivers?
- Are there signals from customers, the market, or internal data that this solution has run its course?
- What do we need to do to responsibly stop and transition customers if we decide to?
- What have we learned from this solution that should inform what we do next?

**Can you move on?** When you've made a clear decision — continue with significant reinvestment (which may mean looping back to earlier questions) or stop. If stopping, capture learnings before closing out.

## How to Use This Framework

**As your internal navigation:**
- Use the questions as your guide to understand where teams are and what they need to validate
- Speak naturally about validation, outcomes, and what needs to be answered
- Never reference questions by number in conversation — not "Q6," not "Question 6," not "the sixth question"
- Instead of "Have you answered Question 3?" say "Have you validated what people are actually trying to accomplish?"
- The question itself is the principle. The number is an internal index and stays internal.
- When you need to show they've skipped a checkpoint: "It sounds like we're exploring concepts before we've validated the customer problem"
- When helping them see a dependency: "We can't effectively answer this without first understanding..."

**As a diagnostic tool:**
- When a team is stuck, internally map to: "Which question are we trying to answer?"
- If they can't answer it with validated knowledge, that's where they need to focus
- Guide them there naturally through questioning, not by lecturing about the framework

**As a navigation tool:**
- Don't skip questions - each builds on the previous
- Moving backward is normal - if concept exploration reveals a misunderstood problem, go back to discovery
- Some questions take longer than others - that's fine

**As an alignment tool:**
- Use it at project kickoff: Help them identify which questions need answering
- Use it mid-project: Surface whether answers are validated or assumed
- Use it to spot misalignment: If some think they're exploring concepts and others think they're still discovering needs, help them align

**Remember:**
- Answers must be validated, not assumed
- If you can't answer a question, iterate to validate before moving forward
- This framework is tool-agnostic - HCD methods are one way to answer these questions, but not the only way

## Relationship to HCD Foundations

If you're using HCD methods to answer these questions:

- **Strategic context (goals; what to work on):** Strategic planning methods, stakeholder alignment
- **Discovering what people are actually trying to accomplish:** Looking methods (empathy interviews, observation, contextual studies)
- **Defining the most impactful problems to solve:** Understanding methods (synthesis, affinity mapping, abstraction laddering, problem framing)
- **Developing solutions and delivering something that works:** Making methods (ideation, prototyping, testing)
- **Post-launch (launch, scale, sustain, sunset):** Launch, scale, sustain methods

The HCD foundations skill provides the philosophy and validation principle. This skill provides the process checkpoints. Together, they give you both the why and the what of innovation work.

## When to Use This Skill

Use this skill when:
- Teams are unclear where they are in the innovation process
- People want to skip ahead before validating earlier questions
- There's misalignment about what's "done" vs what still needs work
- Someone asks "what should we do next?"
- You need a shared language for navigation across different approaches
- You want to diagnose why a project feels stuck

This is a navigation and alignment tool, not a prescriptive methodology. The questions are universal - how you answer them depends on your context and chosen approaches.
