# Prioritization

Align a team on what to work on first by surfacing trade-offs between importance and difficulty, producing a shared road map everyone can get behind.

**Driving question:** How might we focus on the work that creates the greatest value?

---

## Key Question

What should we be working on to achieve our goals?

---

## When to Use

Use this session when a team has a defined set of initiatives, ideas, or opportunities and needs to decide where to invest effort. The right trigger is competing demands with no shared criteria for choosing between them.

Use Bullseye Diagramming as a precursor if the team has more than 12 items — narrow first, then run this session on the survivors. Use Visualize the Vote (dot voting) instead when the decision is low-stakes and a quick read of preferences is enough.

---

## Method Sequence

| Step | Method | Role in this session | Alternative |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 (optional) | Bullseye Diagramming | Narrows a large item set to 8–12 before the matrix | Skip if items ≤ 12 |
| 2 | Importance/Difficulty Matrix | Core prioritization — ranks items on two dimensions, reveals quadrants, produces sequenced road map | Visualize the Vote for lower-stakes decisions |

The Importance/Difficulty Matrix is the session. Bullseye Diagramming is a precursor when the item set is too large. The two methods share a common logic — both are relative ranking activities — which makes the handoff natural when both are used.

---

## Working Session Arc

*Typical duration: 45–60 minutes | Participants: 4–8 people*

### Stage 1 — Frame the Purpose
**Time:** 5 min | **Purpose:** Set context and confirm the items

State the driving question. Confirm that everyone understands the items being prioritized — read each one aloud if needed. Agree on what a successful outcome looks like before starting.

### Stage 2 — Define Importance
**Time:** 5 min | **Purpose:** Establish shared criteria before ranking

Provide or co-create a definition of importance specific to this team's context and goal. Get explicit agreement. Keep the definition visible throughout the session.

### Stage 3 — Rank by Relative Importance
**Time:** 10–15 min | **Purpose:** Produce an importance ranking

Place one item in the center as an anchor. Introduce each remaining item one at a time: "Is this more or less important than what's already placed?" Spread items evenly across the full width when complete. Do not reveal the difficulty axis yet.

### Stage 4 — Define Difficulty
**Time:** 5 min | **Purpose:** Establish shared criteria for the second dimension

Introduce difficulty as a separate dimension. Provide or co-create a definition. Get explicit agreement. Keep the definition visible.

### Stage 5 — Rank by Relative Difficulty
**Time:** 10–15 min | **Purpose:** Add the difficulty dimension

Preserving left-to-right positions, move one item to the center of the vertical axis. Introduce each remaining item: "Is this more or less difficult than what's already placed?" Spread items evenly top to bottom when complete.

### Stage 6 — Reveal the Quadrants
**Time:** 5 min | **Purpose:** Surface the emergent pattern

Reveal the four quadrants. Name them: High ROI (high importance, low difficulty), Strategic Investments (high importance, high difficulty), Quick Wins (low importance, low difficulty), Low Priority (low importance, high difficulty). Ask what the group notices.

### Stage 7 — Sequence the Road Map
**Time:** 10 min | **Purpose:** Translate the matrix into a plan

Start with High ROI items. Suggest a sequence. Connect High ROI items to the Strategic Investments they enable — draw the pathway visibly. Assign owners.

### Stage 8 — Capture Next Steps
**Time:** 5 min | **Purpose:** Close with commitments

Confirm owners and timeframes. Agree to share the matrix with anyone not in the room. Close with feedback: did we achieve what we came here to do?

## Teaching Notes

*Use this section in Teaching mode — when the goal is building someone's capability to run this session.*

**What participants should be able to do afterward:**
- Explain why importance and difficulty must be ranked separately
- Facilitate the importance and difficulty ranking arcs with a live group
- Handle the seven most common stuck points
- Read the quadrant reveal and translate it into a sequenced road map

**Key facilitation moments to teach:**
- The anchor move: always place the first item in the center before asking comparative questions. The most common stumble in practice is asking "where does this go?" without an anchor in place.
- Progressive disclosure: the quadrants are hidden until both axes are ranked. Revealing them early biases the conversation — people argue for quadrant placement instead of having genuine importance and difficulty discussions.
- Separating the dimensions: even when participants know difficulty is coming next, they find it hard to discuss importance in isolation. Facilitators need to hold the line: "We'll get to difficulty — right now let's stay focused on importance relative to our definition."
- The strategic quadrant trap: teams naturally gravitate to High ROI and Quick Wins. The road map is only complete when Strategic Investments have a visible pathway from the current work.

**Warm-up option:**
Run a short What's on Your Radar activity around prioritization before the method — asking why prioritization matters, when it needs to happen, and what makes it challenging. Surfaces existing mental models and builds energy before instruction begins.

**Practice structure:**
Pairs or trios work well. One person facilitates importance, another facilitates difficulty. Switch roles after each run. Structured feedback in the format: what worked, what to try differently, one question.

## Common Questions

**What if we don't know how difficult something is?**
Difficulty here is relative and qualitative, not a precise estimate. Ask the group to compare items to each other rather than rate them absolutely. The conversation about uncertainty is itself part of the output.

**Should the facilitator share their opinion on where items belong?**
If your role is to facilitate, participating compromises your neutrality. If you genuinely want to contribute content, hand the facilitation role to a co-facilitator for that section and name the transition explicitly.

**What do we do about the strategic quadrant — we never seem to get to it?**
The matrix surfaces the problem but doesn't solve it on its own. The road map is what helps: draw explicit connections from High ROI items to the Strategic Investments they enable. Treat Strategic Investments as a horizon — always visible, always being built toward — not a parking lot.

**What if two people disagree on placement and won't move?**
Disagreement is often a sign that two people are using different definitions of importance or difficulty. Step back from the placement and ask each person to say what the dimension means to them for this item. Once the definition is shared, the placement usually resolves itself.
